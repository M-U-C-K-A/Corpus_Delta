# Coastal Erosion: Retreating Shorelines

Coastal erosion is a natural process where shorelines retreat under the action of waves, currents, and wind. Climate change accelerates this phenomenon, threatening infrastructure, homes, and ecosystems.

## Causes of Erosion

### Natural Factors
Wave action is the main erosion agent. Storms, in particular, can set back a cliff by several meters in just hours. Longshore currents move sediments along the coast.

### Human Factors
Sand extraction for construction deprives beaches of their "raw material." Dams retain river sediments that would normally feed coasts. Seawalls and rock armor may protect locally but often displace erosion elsewhere.

### The Role of Climate Change
Sea level rise increases wave height and reach. Storm intensification (discussed but likely) increases major erosion events. Warming also degrades natural defenses like coral reefs and mangroves.

## Consequences

**Land loss**: Some coasts retreat several meters per year. Entire villages have had to be abandoned.

**Infrastructure threat**: Roads, buildings, and historical heritage are endangered.

**Ecosystem loss**: Dunes, coastal wetlands, and salt marshes disappear.

## Solutions

**"Soft" approaches**: Beach nourishment with sand, dune restoration, stabilizing vegetation planting.

**Nature-based solutions**: Coral reef and mangrove restoration as natural barriers.

**Strategic retreat**: In some cases, the most sustainable solution is moving human activities away from the shore rather than fighting the sea.
