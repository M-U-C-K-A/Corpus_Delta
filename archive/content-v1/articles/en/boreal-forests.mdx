# Boreal Forests: The Largest Terrestrial Carbon Sink

The boreal forest, or taiga, forms an immense ring around the Northern Hemisphere, covering Canada, Scandinavia, Russia, and Alaska. It represents about **30% of world forests** and plays a major role in the global carbon cycle.

## A Colossal Carbon Reservoir

The boreal forest stores more carbon than all tropical forests combined—approximately **270-370 billion tonnes of carbon**. Unlike the tropics where carbon is mostly in living biomass, in boreal forests more than 60% of carbon is stored in cold soils and associated peatlands.

## Growing Threats

**Fires**: Boreal forests are naturally shaped by fire, but climate change drastically increases fire frequency and intensity. Summer 2023 in Canada saw more than 18 million hectares burn.

**Pest insects**: The mountain pine beetle and other insects are spreading northward as winters become milder.

**Permafrost thaw**: Taiga soils often rest on permafrost. Thawing destabilizes trees and releases carbon.

## Climate Feedbacks

Warming could transform the boreal forest from a carbon sink to a source. More frequent fires, water stress, and insect infestations could release more carbon than photosynthesis captures.

## Sustainable Management

Reducing global emissions to limit Arctic warming. Adaptive forest management. Protecting high carbon value areas. Fire prevention and management.
