# Climate Sensitivity: The Key Parameter for Projections

Climate sensitivity is the central concept for understanding the magnitude of future warming. It answers a fundamental question: by how much will global average temperature increase if CO₂ concentration doubles?

## Definitions

![Climate Sensitivity Distribution](/articles/climate-sensitivity.png)

### Equilibrium Climate Sensitivity (ECS)
The long-term (centuries) temperature increase after CO₂ doubling, once all climate system adjustments are complete.

**IPCC Range (AR6)**: **2.5°C to 4.0°C** (likely range)
**Central Value**: ~3°C

### Transient Climate Response (TCR)
The temperature increase when CO₂ reaches double, with 1%/year increase. Closer to what we'll experience by 2100.

**IPCC Range**: **1.4°C to 2.2°C**

## Why This Uncertainty?

Uncertainty primarily comes from feedbacks:

### Well-Understood Feedbacks
- **Water vapor**: +1.5 to 2.0 W/m²/K (positive)
- **Ice-snow albedo**: +0.2 to 0.4 W/m²/K (positive)

### Uncertain Feedbacks
- **Clouds**: -0.5 to +1.0 W/m²/K (even the sign is uncertain!)
- **Vegetation**: Variable by region

## Practical Implications

| ECS | Warming 2100 (RCP4.5) | Consequences |
|-----|------------------------|--------------|
| 2.0°C | ~1.5°C | Manageable |
| 3.0°C | ~2.3°C | Difficult |
| 4.5°C | ~3.5°C | Catastrophic |

## Paleoclimatic Constraints

Past climate data help constrain ECS:
- **Last Glacial Maximum** (-5°C, CO₂ at 180 ppm) → ECS ~3°C
- **Pliocene** (+3°C, CO₂ at 400 ppm) → ECS ~2.5-4°C

## What This Means for Action

Even with a "low" ECS of 2.5°C, limiting warming to 1.5°C requires net-zero emissions before 2050. With high ECS, remaining carbon budgets are even smaller.

**Key message**: Uncertainty is not a reason to wait—it's a reason to act with caution.
