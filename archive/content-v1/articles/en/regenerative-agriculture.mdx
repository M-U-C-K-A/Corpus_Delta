# Regenerative Agriculture: When Soils Save the Climate

Regenerative agriculture aims to restore soil health while producing food. It's an approach that can transform agriculture from an emissions source to a carbon sink.

## The Potential of Soils

Soils contain 2-3 times more carbon than the atmosphere. Conventional agriculture has depleted this stock: agricultural soils have lost 50-70% of their organic carbon since being cultivated.

Restoring this carbon could sequester **2-5 Gt CO₂/year**—a significant lever.

## Regenerative Practices

**No-till**: Avoiding turning the soil preserves its structure and carbon.

**Cover crops**: Plants grown between harvests protect the soil, nourish it, and capture carbon.

**Diverse rotation**: Alternating different crops breaks disease cycles and improves fertility.

**Agroforestry**: Integrating trees into farming systems.

**Composting**: Returning organic matter to the soil.

**Reduced chemical inputs**: Favoring soil biological life.

## Multiple Benefits

Beyond carbon, regenerated soils retain water better (drought resilience), require less fertilizer (soil organisms fix nitrogen), resist erosion better, and produce more nutritious food.

## Challenges

The transition takes time (3-5 years to see benefits). Technical knowledge required differs from conventional farming. Institutional support and subsidies still often favor intensive practices.

## Perspectives

More and more farmers and agri-food companies are committing to this path. Agricultural carbon payment systems are emerging but still need refinement.
