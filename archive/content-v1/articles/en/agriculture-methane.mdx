# Agriculture and Methane: The Invisible Emissions

Agriculture is responsible for **10-12% of global direct GHG emissions**, reaching 21-37% if the entire food chain is included. Methane (CH₄), a gas 80 times more potent than CO₂ over 20 years, is the main contributor.

## Agricultural Methane Sources

**Enteric fermentation**: Ruminants (cattle, sheep, goats) produce methane during digestion. One cow emits 70-120 kg CH₄/year. This is the first agricultural source of methane.

**Flooded rice cultivation**: Paddies kept underwater create anaerobic conditions favoring methanogenic bacteria. Rice feeds 3 billion people but emits as much methane as global aviation.

**Manure and slurry**: Storage of animal waste releases methane and nitrous oxide (N₂O).

## The Methane Issue

Methane has a short atmospheric lifetime (12 years vs centuries for CO₂). Reducing methane emissions has a rapid effect on warming—it's a potential climate "quick win."

## Solutions for Livestock

**Improved feed**: Feed additives reducing emissions (seaweed, chemicals like 3-NOP).

**Manure management**: Digesters capturing biogas to produce energy.

**Herd reduction**: Less meat and dairy, more plant proteins.

## Solutions for Rice

**Alternate wetting and drying (AWD)**: Alternating flooding and drying reduces emissions by 30-50%.

**Low-emission varieties**: Genetic selection.

## Sustainable Diets

Switching from a meat-rich diet to a more plant-based one could reduce an individual's food footprint by 50-70%.
