# Urban Heat Islands: What Are We Talking About?

During heatwaves, temperatures in the city can be 5 to 10°C higher than in the surrounding countryside. This phenomenon, called "urban heat island" (UHI), poses serious public health and comfort problems.

## The Causes

### Concrete and Asphalt
Urban materials absorb heat from the sun during the day and release it at night, preventing the city from cooling down. Soil sealing also limits water evaporation, which is a natural cooling mechanism.

### Human Activity
Car traffic, air conditioning, and industries generate anthropogenic heat that adds to solar warming.

### Lack of Vegetation
Trees and green spaces act as natural air conditioners thanks to shading and evapotranspiration. Their scarcity in the city accentuates the heat island effect.

## Consequences

UHIs increase the risks of dehydration and heatstroke, particularly among the elderly and children. They also lead to overconsumption of energy for air conditioning, creating a vicious circle (AC releases heat outside).

## Solutions: Greening the City

To cool our cities, we must:
- **Multiply parks and gardens**.
- **Green roofs and facades**.
- **Use reflective materials** (instead of black bitumen) for roads and roofs.
- **Unseal soils** to allow water to infiltrate.

Rethinking urban planning is essential to adapt our cities to global warming.
