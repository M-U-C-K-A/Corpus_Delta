# Fast Fashion: The True Price of Our Clothes

"Fast Fashion" refers to the ultra-rapid turnover of clothing collections at very low prices. This business model has disastrous environmental and social consequences.

## A Heavy Carbon Footprint

The textile industry is one of the most polluting in the world. It emits more greenhouse gases than international flights and maritime traffic combined. The production of synthetic fibers (polyester) relies on oil, while conventional cotton consumes huge amounts of water and pesticides.

## Water Pollution

Textile dyes and chemical treatments release toxic substances into rivers, often in countries where regulations are weak. In addition, washing synthetic clothes releases microplastics that end up in the oceans.

## Waste and Disposal

The poor quality of clothes encourages overconsumption and disposability. Tons of textiles end up in open landfills or are incinerated every year.

## Working Conditions

The pressure to produce fast and cheap affects workers, who are often underpaid and work in dangerous conditions.

## Towards Ethical Fashion

The consumer has immense power. Buying less but better, favoring second-hand items, choosing eco-friendly materials, and supporting ethical brands are concrete actions to counter Fast Fashion.
