# Marine Heatwaves: Underwater Heat Waves

Marine heatwaves (MHWs) are prolonged periods of abnormally high ocean temperatures. Long ignored, they are now recognized as major climate events with devastating impacts.

## Scientific Definition

An MHW is defined as a period of at least **5 consecutive days** where surface temperature exceeds the **90th percentile** of historical temperatures for that period and region.

MHWs are classified by intensity, from "Moderate" to "Extreme" (nicknamed "beyond extreme").

## Notable Events

**"The Blob" (2013-2015)** — A 1,600 km wide mass of warm water in the Northeast Pacific persisted for over two years, causing massive mortality of seabirds and marine mammals.

**Mediterranean (2022)** — Anomalies of +5°C were measured, causing mass mortality of gorgonians and other sessile invertebrates.

## Ecological Impacts

MHWs cause coral bleaching, mass mortality of invertebrates and kelp, forced species migrations toward cooler waters, and toxic algal blooms.

## Link to Climate Change

MHWs have become more frequent (doubling since 1982), longer (+17% duration), more intense (+17% temperature), and more extensive (50% more area affected).

## Economic Impacts

MHWs affect fisheries through mortality or migration of stocks, and aquaculture through mass mortality. In 2015-2016, the Pacific MHW caused losses of hundreds of millions of dollars for the seafood industry.

## Adaptation

Development of early warning systems, fishery diversification, selection of more resistant aquaculture strains, and protection of natural thermal refuges.
