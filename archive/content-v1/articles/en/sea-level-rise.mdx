# Sea Level Rise: A Growing Threat

Sea level rise is one of the most inexorable consequences of climate change. It threatens hundreds of millions of people living in coastal areas and small island states.

## Sources of Rise

![Sea Level Rise Projections](/articles/sea-level-rise.png)

Sea level rises for three main reasons:

### Thermal Expansion
Seawater expands as it warms. This phenomenon accounts for about **40%** of observed rise since 1993. The ocean has absorbed more than 90% of excess heat from warming.

### Mountain Glacier Melting
Glaciers in the Alps, Himalayas, Andes, and Alaska are losing mass at an accelerating rate. Their contribution is approximately **25%** of current rise.

### Polar Ice Sheets
Greenland and Antarctica together contain enough ice to raise sea level by **65 meters**. Their melting is accelerating and now represents about **35%** of rise.

## Recent Observations

Since 1901, global mean sea level has risen by about **20 cm**. The rate of rise has doubled since 1993, reaching **3.7 mm/year** between 2006-2018.

## Future Projections

Projections depend heavily on future emissions. By 2100, the IPCC projects a rise of **43-84 cm** depending on warming scenario. Beyond 2100, rise could exceed several meters if Greenland and Antarctic ice sheets collapse.

## Most Vulnerable Regions

Densely populated deltas like Bangladesh, Vietnam, and Egypt are particularly at risk. Small Pacific island states (Tuvalu, Kiribati, Maldives) face an existential threat.

## Solutions and Adaptation

**Mitigation**: Reduce emissions to limit warming and therefore rise.

**Adaptation**: Seawalls and coastal barriers, mangrove and wetland restoration, urban planning avoiding at-risk zones, and in extreme cases, planned population relocation.
