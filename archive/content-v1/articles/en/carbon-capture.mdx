# Carbon Capture and Storage: The Technology of Last Resort?

Carbon capture and storage (CCS) involves capturing CO₂ at the source or from the atmosphere and storing it durably. It's a controversial but potentially necessary technology.

## Types of Capture

**Point source capture**: CO₂ is captured at industrial or power plant chimneys. Mature but expensive technology.

**Direct air capture (DAC)**: Machines draw ambient air and extract CO₂ via chemical sorbents. Very energy-intensive but applicable anywhere.

**BECCS**: Bioenergy with carbon capture and storage. Burning biomass (which captured CO₂ while growing) then capturing emissions = net negative emissions.

## Storage

Captured CO₂ must be stored for millennia. Options include:

**Geological formations**: Deep saline aquifers, depleted oil or gas fields. Global capacity estimated at thousands of GtCO₂.

**Mineralization**: CO₂ reacts with certain rocks (basalt) to form stable carbonates. Carbfix project in Iceland.

## State of the Technology

About 40 Mt CO₂/year are currently captured and stored, less than 0.1% of annual emissions. The net-zero goal would require gigatons per year.

## Limitations and Criticisms

**Cost**: $100-600/tCO₂ for DAC, $50-100/t for industrial capture.

**Energy**: DAC requires enormous amounts of clean energy.

**Moral hazard risk**: Justifying continued fossil fuel use.

## Role in the Transition

CCS cannot replace emissions reduction, but it can decarbonize "difficult" sectors (cement, steel) and potentially remove historical emissions. It's a complement, not a silver bullet.
