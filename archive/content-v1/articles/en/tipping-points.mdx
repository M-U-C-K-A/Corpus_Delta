# Tipping Points: When Climate Tips Over

Tipping points are thresholds beyond which rapid and potentially irreversible changes occur in the climate system. Crossing them could commit the planet to an uncontrollable trajectory.

## What is a Tipping Point?

It's a threshold where a small additional change triggers a major self-sustaining transformation. Like a snowball that, once rolling, grows on its own.

## Major Tipping Points

**Greenland ice sheet**: From 1.5-2°C warming, melting could become self-sustaining. Sea level rise of 7 meters over centuries.

**West Antarctic ice sheet**: Key glaciers already show signs of instability. Potential for 3-5 meters of rise.

**Amazon rainforest**: Between deforestation and droughts, risk of transformation to savanna. Loss of a major carbon sink.

**Permafrost**: Thawing releasing methane and CO₂, amplifying warming.

**Thermohaline circulation**: Slowdown already observed, risk of Gulf Stream collapse.

**Indian monsoon**: Possible destabilization with consequences for 1.5 billion people.

## Cascading Interactions

Most concerning: tipping points can trigger each other. Greenland melting could disrupt Atlantic circulation, which would affect the African monsoon, etc.

## Implications

These risks justify urgent and ambitious climate action. Exceeding 1.5°C significantly increases the probability of crossing multiple tipping points.
