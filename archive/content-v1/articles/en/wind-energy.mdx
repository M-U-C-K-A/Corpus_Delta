# Wind Energy: Harnessing the Winds of Change

Wind power is one of the key technologies of the energy transition. Onshore or offshore, it provides abundant low-carbon electricity, complementary to solar.

## Sector Growth

Global wind capacity exceeds 1,000 GW. Onshore wind still represents the majority, but offshore wind is growing rapidly.

## How It Works

Blades capture the kinetic energy of wind and spin a generator. Modern turbines reach 200 m in height and 15 MW of power (offshore). A single offshore turbine can power more than 10,000 homes.

## Costs and Competitiveness

Costs have dropped 70% since 2009 for onshore wind. Onshore wind is often the cheapest source of electricity. Offshore, though more expensive, benefits from stronger, more regular winds.

## Offshore Wind

Offshore parks avoid land constraints and benefit from stronger, more regular winds. Floating offshore enables exploitation of deep waters (Mediterranean, Atlantic coasts).

## Grid Integration

Wind variability (no electricity without wind) requires interconnected grids, storage, and backup capacity. Geographic complementarity (it's always windy somewhere) helps.

## Environmental Impact

Low lifecycle emissions. Questions about bird and bat impacts (improving), noise (distance from residences), and landscape (frequent local opposition).

## Future Potential

The IEA estimates wind could provide more than 35% of global electricity by 2050.
