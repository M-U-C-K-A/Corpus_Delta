# The Albedo Effect: The Ice-Albedo Feedback Loop

Albedo is the measure of a surface's reflectivity. This parameter plays a crucial role in Earth's energy balance and constitutes one of the most powerful climate feedbacks.

## What is Albedo?

![Albedo Effect Diagram](/articles/albedo-effect.png)

Albedo is expressed as a coefficient between 0 (total absorption) and 1 (total reflection):

| Surface | Albedo |
|---------|--------|
| Fresh snow | 0.80 - 0.90 |
| Sea ice | 0.50 - 0.70 |
| Ocean (high sun) | 0.06 |
| Forest | 0.10 - 0.20 |
| Desert | 0.30 - 0.40 |

## The Ice-Albedo Feedback

This is one of the most important positive feedbacks in the climate system:

1. **Initial warming** → Temperature increases
2. **Ice melting** → Ice/snow surface decreases
3. **Albedo decrease** → Less radiation reflected
4. **Increased absorption** → Surface absorbs more energy
5. **Amplified warming** → Return to step 1

This loop explains why the Arctic is warming **2 to 3 times faster** than the global average ("Arctic amplification").

## Observational Data

### Arctic Sea Ice Decline
- **1979**: Minimum extent ~7 million km²
- **2012**: Record minimum of 3.4 million km²
- **2020s**: Trend at 4-5 million km²
- **Projection**: Ice-free summer possible before 2050

### Impact on Radiative Balance
Arctic sea ice loss adds an estimated radiative forcing of **0.2-0.5 W/m²**, equivalent to years of CO₂ emissions.

## Other Albedo Effects

### Soot on Snow
Black carbon deposits on snow reduce its albedo from 0.85 to 0.70, accelerating melting.

### Urbanization
Replacing vegetation with dark surfaces (asphalt, roofs) reduces local albedo and contributes to urban heat islands.

## Solutions

1. **Reduce soot emissions**: Filters, clean combustion
2. **White roofs and reflective surfaces**: Local geoengineering
3. **Protect polar regions**: Limit global warming
