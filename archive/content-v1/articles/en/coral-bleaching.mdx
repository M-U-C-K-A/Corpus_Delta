# Coral Bleaching: The Agony of the Reefs

Coral reefs, often called "rainforests of the sea," harbor 25% of all marine species. They are now on the front line of climate change, experiencing increasingly frequent and severe bleaching events.

## The Bleaching Mechanism

![Coral Bleaching Process](/articles/coral-bleaching.png)

Corals live in symbiosis with microscopic algae called **zooxanthellae**. These algae provide up to 90% of the coral's energy through photosynthesis and give it its characteristic colors.

When water becomes too warm (generally **+1°C above the usual summer maximum** for 4 weeks), the stressed coral expels its zooxanthellae. The coral turns white—"bleached"—and, deprived of its energy source, can die if the stress persists.

## Major Bleaching Events

Australia's Great Barrier Reef has experienced mass bleaching in 1998, 2002, 2016, 2017, 2020, and 2022. In 2016, 29% of surface corals died within months. These events, once rare, are now becoming annual.

## Ecological Impacts

Coral death leads to collapse of the entire reef ecosystem, including the disappearance of reef fish and dependent species. Reefs also provide coastal protection by breaking waves, protection that disappears with them.

## Economic Impacts

Coral reefs generate approximately **$36 billion per year** in tourism and fishing. More than 500 million people depend directly on reefs for food and income.

## Future of Reefs

With +1.5°C warming, the IPCC estimates 70-90% of coral reefs will disappear. At +2°C, it would be over 99%. Without drastic climate action, coral reefs as we know them could vanish by 2050.

## Solutions

**Emission reduction** (the only long-term solution), creating marine protected areas, reducing local stresses (pollution, overfishing), and research on coral resilience and active restoration.
