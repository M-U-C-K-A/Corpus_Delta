# Industrial Emissions: The Core of the Climate Problem

Industry represents about **21% of global direct greenhouse gas emissions**, and much more if electricity consumed is included. Decarbonizing industry is one of the most difficult challenges of the transition.

## The Big Emitters

**Steel**: Steel production emits about 2.6 Gt CO₂/year, or 7% of global emissions. The dominant process uses coal as a reducing agent in blast furnaces.

**Cement**: 2.4 Gt CO₂/year (6% global). CO₂ comes both from combustion to heat limestone and from the chemical calcination reaction itself.

**Chemicals**: Ammonia (for fertilizers), plastics, and other chemicals represent about 5% of emissions. Hydrogen used comes from methane reforming.

**Aluminum**: Very energy-intensive production, often coal-powered in China.

## Why It's Difficult

Unlike electricity (renewables) or transport (electrification), heavy industry often requires very high temperatures and chemical reactions that inherently release CO₂. Investments are heavy and renewal cycles long (40-50 year plants).

## Technological Solutions

**Green hydrogen**: Replace coal and methane with hydrogen produced by renewable electrolysis.

**Electrification**: Electric furnaces for recycled steel, electric heating.

**Carbon capture and storage (CCS)**: Capture CO₂ at plant exit and store it geologically.

**Circular economy**: Massive recycling to reduce primary production.

## Policy Levers

Low-carbon product standards, carbon border adjustment (CBAM), green public procurement, and support for industrial innovation.
