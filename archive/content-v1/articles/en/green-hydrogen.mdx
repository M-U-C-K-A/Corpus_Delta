# Green Hydrogen: Energy Carrier of the Future?

Hydrogen is presented as a centerpiece of the energy transition. But everything depends on its color—that is, how it's produced.

## The Colors of Hydrogen

**Grey**: Produced by natural gas reforming. 95% of current production. Very emitting (10 kg CO₂ / kg H₂).

**Blue**: Grey hydrogen with CO₂ capture. Partial emissions reduction. Controversial transitional solution.

**Green**: Produced by water electrolysis powered by renewable electricity. Zero emissions. Still high cost but declining.

## Promising Applications

**Heavy industry**: Replace coal in blast furnaces for "green" steel. Decarbonize ammonia production.

**Heavy transport**: Long-distance trucks, trains on non-electrified lines, ferries, potentially aviation.

**Energy storage**: Store surplus renewable electricity as hydrogen for periods without wind or sun.

**Heating**: Injection into existing gas networks, mixed with methane then potentially pure.

## Challenges to Overcome

**Efficiency**: The electricity → hydrogen → use chain loses 50-70% of initial energy. Direct electrification is almost always preferable when possible.

**Infrastructure**: Pipelines, distribution stations, storage: everything needs to be built.

**Cost**: Currently 4-6 €/kg for green vs 1-2 €/kg for grey.

## Perspectives

Projections estimate hydrogen could represent 10-20% of the global energy mix by 2050. But it remains a niche carrier, concentrated on uses where electrification is impossible.
