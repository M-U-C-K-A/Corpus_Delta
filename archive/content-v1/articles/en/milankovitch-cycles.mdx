# Milankovitch Cycles: The Metronome of Ice Ages

Milankovitch cycles are periodic variations in Earth's orbit and rotational axis that modulate the amount and distribution of solar radiation received. These cycles explain the rhythm of Quaternary glaciations.

## The Three Orbital Parameters

![Milankovitch Cycles](/articles/milankovitch-cycles.png)

### 1. Eccentricity (100,000 and 400,000 years)
Earth's orbit oscillates between nearly circular (e ≈ 0.005) and slightly elliptical (e ≈ 0.058).

**Effect**: Modifies Earth-Sun distance and thus radiation intensity received (up to ±23%).

### 2. Obliquity (41,000 years)
The tilt of the rotational axis varies between 22.1° and 24.5° (currently 23.4°).

**Effect**: Higher obliquity amplifies seasonal contrasts. Warmer summers at high latitudes favor ice melting.

### 3. Precession (23,000 years)
The rotational axis describes a cone (like a top) and perihelion/aphelion dates shift in the calendar.

**Effect**: Determines which hemisphere has summers at perihelion (closer to the Sun).

## The Glaciation Mechanism

Milankovitch theory states that **cool summers at Northern Hemisphere high latitudes** trigger glaciations:

1. **Low obliquity + Perihelion in NH winter** → Cool summers in Northern Hemisphere
2. **Persistent snow** → Beginning of ice accumulation
3. **Albedo feedback** → Amplification of cooling
4. **Ice sheet expansion** → Ice age

## Validation by Data

Antarctic ice cores (Vostok, EPICA) show remarkable correlation between:
- 100,000-year cycles and glacial/interglacial alternation
- 41,000-year cycles dominant before 1 million years ago

## Milankovitch and Current Warming

**Frequent question**: "Isn't current warming due to Milankovitch cycles?"

**Answer**: No. Orbital cycles predict slow cooling toward the next glaciation (in ~50,000 years). Current warming is 10 times faster and opposite in direction to orbital forcing.

## Future Implications

Without human intervention, the next glacial cycle would occur in ~50,000 years. However, anthropogenic CO₂ emissions could delay the next glaciation by hundreds of thousands of years.
