# Glacier Retreat: Silent Witnesses of Warming

Mountain glaciers are among the most visible and sensitive indicators of climate change. Their widespread retreat across all continents testifies to the magnitude of warming.

## Global Observations

Since 1850, Alpine glaciers have lost more than **60% of their volume**. This trend is accelerating: the loss rate has doubled since 2000. The Aletsch Glacier (Switzerland), the largest in the Alps, has retreated more than 3 km and lost 250 m in thickness.

## Affected Regions

**European Alps**: Some glaciers could disappear before 2100.

**Himalaya-Karakoram**: Contrasting situation, but overall net loss threatening water supply for 1.5 billion people.

**Tropical Andes**: Dramatic retreat of Bolivian and Peruvian glaciers, threatening water for Andean cities.

**Africa**: Kilimanjaro and Mount Kenya glaciers have lost more than 80% of their area.

## Consequences

**Water resources**: Glaciers regulate river flow. In summer, when rains are lacking, it's glacial melt that feeds rivers. Without glaciers, summer supply will be drastically reduced.

**Natural hazards**: Unstable glacial lakes (GLOFs), serac falls, slope destabilization.

**Tourism and heritage**: Loss of iconic landscapes and related economic activities.

## Irreversibility

Even if we stopped emissions today, much of mountain glaciers would continue retreating for decades due to climate inertia. This reality makes adaptation as crucial as mitigation.
