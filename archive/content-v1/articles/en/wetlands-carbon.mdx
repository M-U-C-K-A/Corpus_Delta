# Wetlands: The Giants of Carbon Storage

Wetlands—peatlands, marshes, wet meadows—cover only 3% of land but store approximately **30% of terrestrial carbon**. Their protection is crucial for climate.

## Peatlands: Super-Storers

Peatlands form when dead vegetation doesn't fully decompose in water-saturated, oxygen-poor conditions. Organic matter accumulates over millennia, sometimes forming several meters of peat.

One hectare of peatland can store **10 times more carbon** than a hectare of tropical forest. Indonesian tropical peatlands and Canadian and Russian boreal peatlands are particularly important.

## Threats to Wetlands

**Drainage for agriculture**: In Europe, more than 50% of peatlands have been drained. Once drained, peat oxidizes and releases its carbon as CO₂.

**Peat fires**: Drained peatlands can catch fire and burn for months, releasing massive amounts of carbon. Indonesian fires in 2015 emitted more CO₂ than Japan's economy in a year.

**Peat extraction**: For horticulture and heating.

## Other Services

Flood regulation (natural sponge), water purification, habitat for unique biodiversity, and valuable paleoclimate archives.

## Solutions

Stop drainage of intact wetlands. "Rewetting" of drained peatlands to halt emissions. Paludiculture: farming compatible with wet soils. Substituting horticultural peat with sustainable alternatives.
