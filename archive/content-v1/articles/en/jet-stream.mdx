# Jet Stream: The Atmospheric River in Crisis

The polar jet stream is a fast-moving air current flowing west to east at about 10 km altitude, at the boundary between cold Arctic air and warm temperate air. Its behavior directly influences weather conditions in the Northern Hemisphere.

## How the Jet Stream Works

![Polar Jet Stream](/articles/jet-stream.png)

### Structure
- **Altitude**: 9-12 km (tropopause)
- **Speed**: 150-300 km/h (sometimes > 400 km/h)
- **Width**: ~100 km
- **Driver**: Equator-pole temperature gradient

### Normal Behavior
The jet stream naturally undulates, forming ridges (northward) and troughs (southward). These undulations, called **Rossby waves**, move from west to east, carrying weather systems along.

## The Arctic Amplification Hypothesis

The Arctic is warming 2-4 times faster than the rest of the globe. This "Arctic amplification" reduces the equator-pole temperature gradient, which:

1. **Weakens the jet stream**: Less thermal "fuel"
2. **Amplifies undulations**: More pronounced meanders
3. **Slows progression**: Waves "block" more often
4. **Increased persistence**: Weather patterns last longer

## Weather Consequences

### Cold Waves
When the jet stream plunges southward, it brings Arctic air to mid-latitudes:
- **Texas 2021**: -18°C, energy crisis
- **Europe 2018**: "Beast from the East"

### Heat Waves
When the jet stream retreats northward, subtropical hot air invades temperate latitudes:
- **Canada heat dome 2021**: 49.6°C at Lytton
- **European heatwaves 2019, 2022**

### Persistent Blocking
Quasi-stationary waves cause **prolonged droughts** or **repeated flooding** in the same location.

## Scientific Debate

This theory is still debated:
- **For**: Observed correlations, plausible physical mechanisms
- **Against**: Signal difficult to separate from natural variability

## Solutions and Adaptation

1. **Improve forecasts**: Develop models that better capture blocking
2. **Warning systems**: Prevent extreme events
3. **Reduce emissions**: Limit Arctic amplification
