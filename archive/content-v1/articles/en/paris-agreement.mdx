# The Paris Agreement: Pillar of Global Climate Action

Adopted in December 2015 at COP21, the Paris Agreement is the first universal and legally binding climate treaty. It marks a turning point in international cooperation.

## Main Objectives

**Limit warming** to well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels, and pursue efforts to limit it to 1.5°C.

**Strengthen adaptation capacity** of countries to climate change impacts.

**Align financial flows** with low-carbon, climate-resilient development.

## How It Works

### Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs)
Each country submits its emissions reduction commitments (NDCs). These commitments are voluntary but the submission process is mandatory.

### Ratchet Mechanism
NDCs must be revised upward every 5 years. This "ratchet" aims for increasing ambition.

### Transparency
A common transparency framework ensures progress is measured and reported comparably.

## The Reality: Insufficient Commitments

Despite the agreement, current cumulative NDCs lead to warming of about 2.5-2.9°C by 2100. The gap between promises and actions remains significant.

## Global Stocktake

Every 5 years, a "Global Stocktake" assesses collective progress. The first, in 2023, confirmed the world is not on a 1.5°C trajectory.

## Strengths and Limitations

**Strengths**: Universality, flexibility, momentum of increasing ambition.

**Limitations**: No sanctions, dependence on national political will, insufficient climate finance ($100 billion/year promises rarely met).
