# Carbon Pricing: Putting a Price on Pollution

Carbon pricing is the central economic tool for internalizing the cost of CO₂. By making pollution costly, it incentivizes businesses and consumers to reduce their emissions.

## Two Main Approaches

**Carbon tax**: A fixed price per tonne of CO₂ emitted. Simple, predictable, but the effect on emissions is uncertain.

Sweden has the highest carbon tax: ~€120/tCO₂ on fuels outside ETS. France: ~€45/tCO₂.

**Cap-and-trade (ETS)**: The government sets an emissions cap and distributes "pollution rights" that companies can trade. Price varies with supply and demand.

The EU ETS covers ~40% of EU emissions. 2023-2024 price: €50-100/tCO₂.

## Optimal Price

Economists estimate a price of **€100-250/tCO₂** would be necessary to meet Paris goals. We're far from this in most jurisdictions.

## Revenue Redistribution

For social acceptability, revenues can be redistributed via a "carbon dividend" (uniform check to all citizens), reduction of other taxes, transition financing (renovation, transport), or aid to vulnerable households.

## Carbon Border Adjustment

The EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) taxes imports based on their carbon content. This prevents "carbon leakage" to less ambitious countries.

## Limitations

Pricing alone isn't enough. Regulations (standards, bans), public investment, and innovation support are also needed.
