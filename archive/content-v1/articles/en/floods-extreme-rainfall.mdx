# Floods: Too Much Water, Too Fast

Extreme precipitation and floods are among the deadliest and most costly natural disasters. Climate change is intensifying these events in measurable ways.

## Why More Extreme Rainfall?

Warmer atmosphere can hold more water vapor: about **7% more per degree of warming** (Clausius-Clapeyron relationship). This extra moisture translates into more intense precipitation.

## Recent Events

**Germany and Belgium (July 2021)**: More than 180 deaths, towns devastated. Attribution analysis showed warming made this event 1.2 to 9 times more likely.

**Pakistan (August 2022)**: A third of the country underwater, 1700 deaths, 33 million affected. Exceptional monsoon amplified by warming.

**Libya (September 2023)**: Dam failures after extreme rainfall, more than 6000 deaths in Derna.

## Types of Flooding

**River flooding**: Watercourse overflow after prolonged rains.

**Flash floods**: Very rapid water rise after intense localized rains, the deadliest type.

**Coastal flooding**: Combination of storms and high tides, amplified by sea level rise.

**Urban runoff**: Soil impermeabilization preventing infiltration.

## Adaptation

Early warning systems, flood zone mapping, urban planning respecting flood plains, soil desealing, wetland restoration, and protective infrastructure (levees, retention basins).
