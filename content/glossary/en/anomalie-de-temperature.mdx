## Why a departure rather than a temperature

Absolute temperatures vary enormously from one station to the next, with altitude,
distance from the sea or exposure. Averaging them directly would produce a figure governed
by where the stations happen to be rather than by the climate.

**Departures from a local mean**, by contrast, behave far more coherently across wide
regions: when a warm air mass settles in, stations across an area depart from their normal
in the same direction, whatever their absolute temperature. That is what makes a reliable
global average possible.

The reference period must always be stated: the same year shows a different anomaly
depending on whether it is measured against 1951-1980, 1961-1990, or the pre-industrial era.
