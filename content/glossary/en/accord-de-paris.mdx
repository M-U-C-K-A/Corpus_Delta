The agreement rests on an unusual mechanism: reduction targets are not imposed. Each state
declares its own — its "nationally determined contributions" — and undertakes to revise
them upwards at regular intervals.

That choice explains both its near-universal ratification and its structural weakness:
nothing compels a state to set an ambitious target, and nothing penalises it for missing one.

<Callout variant="info" title="A global, multi-year average">
"1.5 °C" refers to a worldwide average over several years, measured against the
pre-industrial period. A single year above that threshold therefore does not mean the goal
has been missed — any more than a single year below it means the goal has been met.
</Callout>
