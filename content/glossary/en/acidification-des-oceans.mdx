## The other side of the ocean sink

As it dissolves CO₂, seawater forms carbonic acid and its pH falls. The service the ocean
provides — absorbing a large share of emissions — therefore carries a direct chemical
price, one that has nothing to do with temperature.

The main biological consequence falls on calcifying organisms: corals, molluscs, some
plankton. A more acidic medium makes building their skeletons or shells more costly in
energy, and can dissolve existing structures.

<Callout variant="caution" title="Acidification does not mean an acidic ocean">
Seawater remains alkaline. The term describes a **shift towards acidity**, not the crossing
of a threshold. Because pH is a logarithmic scale, a seemingly small change corresponds to
a substantial change in hydrogen ion concentration.
</Callout>
