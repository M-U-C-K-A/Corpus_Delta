## The longest direct measurement

In 1958 Charles David Keeling installed a CO₂ analyser at Mauna Loa Observatory, 3,400
metres up a Hawaiian volcano, far from any industrial source. The measurement has not stopped since.
It is the longest continuous record of atmospheric CO₂ concentration, and it settled a then-open
question: do human emissions actually accumulate in the air, or does the ocean absorb them as they
come?

The curve has two components. An **annual oscillation** of about 6 ppm, which is the breathing of
northern hemisphere vegetation: CO₂ falls in spring and summer as plants grow, rises in autumn as
leaves decay. And a **continuous climb**, from 315 ppm in 1958 to over 425 ppm today.

Its value lies in modelling nothing. One instrument, one place, one method unchanged for sixty-five
years: it is an observation, and that is what makes it hard to dispute.
