A glacier lives on a balance: what it gains as snow on one side, what it loses to melt on
the other. When losses prevail for long enough, it retreats — a geometric fact that can be
observed without any statistical treatment, which makes it one of the hardest indicators to
dispute.

Beyond sea level, mountain glaciers play a major hydrological role: they store water in
winter and release it in summer, precisely when rainfall is scarce. Their disappearance
does not remove that water, it removes its **seasonal regulation**, which weighs on
irrigation and hydropower downstream.
