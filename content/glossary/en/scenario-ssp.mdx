## Assumptions, not predictions

A climate model cannot guess the political and economic decisions of the coming decades. It
is therefore given explicit assumptions: that is the role of the SSP scenarios, used since
the IPCC's sixth assessment report.

Their notation carries two pieces of information. The first digit denotes the
socio-economic trajectory — demographics, development, international cooperation, reliance
on fossil fuels. The number that follows gives the
<Term id="forcage-radiatif">radiative forcing</Term> reached in 2100, in W/m². So `SSP1-2.6`
pairs a world oriented towards sustainability with a forcing of 2.6 W/m², while `SSP5-8.5`
combines fossil-fuelled growth with a forcing of 8.5 W/m².

<Callout variant="caution" title="A scenario is not a forecast">
Comparing scenarios means comparing assumptions, not likely futures. SSP5-8.5 in particular
is often wrongly presented as the "business as usual" case: it was designed as an upper
bound, useful for testing how models respond.
</Callout>
