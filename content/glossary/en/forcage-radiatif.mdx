## The common unit of climatic perturbations

Radiative forcing quantifies how much a perturbation alters Earth's energy balance, in
W/m². A positive forcing adds energy to the system and tends to warm it; a negative one
removes energy.

Its main value is to **reduce phenomena of very different natures to a comparable
quantity**: the accumulation of CO₂, methane emissions, industrial aerosols, variations in
solar activity or a major volcanic eruption are all expressed in the same unit. That is
what makes it possible to state that some contributions weigh far more heavily than others,
rather than listing them without hierarchy.

Combined with <Term id="sensibilite-climatique">climate sensitivity</Term>, radiative
forcing links a physical cause to an expected amount of warming.
