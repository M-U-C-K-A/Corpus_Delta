Two distinct mechanisms combine. In the open ocean, warm water dissolves less oxygen than
cold water, and surface warming strengthens stratification: the layers mix less, so
atmospheric oxygen reaches the depths less readily. Near coasts, nutrient inputs from
farming and cities trigger algal blooms whose decomposition consumes the available oxygen.

Areas where the concentration falls too low to sustain animal life are called dead zones.
They force mobile species to move and eliminate the rest, contributing to the general
redistribution of marine life.
