## The region moving fastest

The Arctic does not warm like the rest of the planet: since 1979 it has gained close to
**four times** the global mean change. The gap is not a measurement artefact but an expected
consequence of the region's physics.

The main mechanism is an <Term id="albedo">albedo</Term>
<Term id="retroaction-climatique">feedback</Term>. <Term id="banquise">Sea ice</Term> and snow
reflect most incoming sunlight; the ocean and bare ground they uncover absorb it. Each melt
therefore exposes a more absorbent surface, which speeds the next melt. Added to this are a thin,
strongly stratified polar atmosphere that concentrates warming near the ground, and increased heat
transport from mid-latitudes.

The amplification does not stay local. It weakens the pole-to-equator temperature contrast that
drives the <Term id="courant-jet">jet stream</Term>, and it thaws
<Term id="permafrost">permafrost</Term>, whose soils release long-immobilised carbon.
