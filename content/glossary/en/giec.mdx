## What the IPCC does, and what it does not

The IPCC produces no research and takes no measurements. It **assesses** the published
literature and draws from it a state of knowledge, organised into three working groups: the
physical basis, impacts and adaptation, and mitigation.

Its distinctive feature is its adoption procedure. The summaries for policymakers are
approved line by line by representatives of member states, in the presence of the authors.
That political validation is the price of the document's authority — and its main recurring
criticism, since the search for consensus tends to produce cautious wording.

Its vocabulary of uncertainty is codified: "likely", "very likely" and "virtually certain"
correspond to defined probability ranges, not to literary judgements.
