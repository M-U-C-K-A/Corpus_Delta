## A carbon store held in place by cold

The definition of permafrost turns on **temperature**, not on ice: dry rocky ground
permanently below 0 °C is permafrost. It covers a substantial share of the northern
hemisphere's high latitudes.

Its climatic significance lies in what it contains. Millennia of plant matter have piled up
there without decomposing, the cold having halted microbial activity. Thaw restarts that
decomposition and releases carbon dioxide and methane, gases which in turn sharpen the
warming.

This feedback is one of the reasons permafrost is regularly listed among the climate
system's potential <Term id="point-de-bascule">tipping points</Term>.
