## Treating the symptom, not the cause

The term covers two families better kept apart.
<Term id="emissions-negatives">Negative emissions</Term> address the cause by removing CO₂. **Solar
radiation management** does not touch CO₂ at all: it reflects a fraction of incoming sunlight, for
instance by injecting sulphate <Term id="aerosols">aerosols</Term> into the stratosphere, in
imitation of a major volcanic eruption.

The second family draws the objections. It would act fast and at modest technical cost, but would
leave CO₂ concentration and <Term id="acidification-des-oceans">ocean acidification</Term>
untouched. It would shift rainfall unevenly between regions. And it would create dependence: an
abrupt halt after decades of injection would produce very rapid warming, the **termination shock**.

On top of this sits an unprecedented governance problem — the capability would be within reach of a
single state, for a planet-wide effect. The IPCC treats it as a last-resort option, documented but
not recommended.
