## A distinction that skews many comparisons

Coal, oil, uranium and sunlight are primary energy. The electricity or fuel that reaches
the user is final energy. Between the two, conversion losses are considerable: a thermal
power station dissipates a large share as heat.

This difference explains a counter-intuitive result: electrifying a use can cut **primary**
energy consumption without changing the service delivered at all, because an electric motor
converts far more efficiently than the thermal chain it replaces.

Comparing two energy systems therefore requires stating which of the two quantities is
being used. The same transition can look spectacular or modest depending on that choice.
