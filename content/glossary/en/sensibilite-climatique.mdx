## The parameter that governs all the others

Climate sensitivity answers a simple question: if CO₂ concentration doubles, by how much
does the global mean temperature rise once the system has returned to equilibrium? It is
the parameter that turns a physical cause into a thermal consequence, and it governs the
magnitude of almost every projection.

In its sixth assessment report, the IPCC adopts a **central estimate of 3 °C**, with a
likely range of 2.5 to 4 °C. That assessment does not rest on models alone: it draws on
three independent families of evidence — the physics of feedbacks, the climate observed
since the industrial era, and the climates of the geological past.

<Callout variant="uncertainty" title="An uncertainty that does not fully close">
Narrowing this range is one of the notable advances of recent decades, but the remaining
uncertainty is not a flaw in method: it reflects, among other things, the persistent
difficulty of representing cloud feedbacks.
</Callout>
