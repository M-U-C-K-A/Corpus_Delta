## From the global grid to the valley

A <Term id="modele-climatique">global model</Term> cuts the planet into cells about a
hundred kilometres across. At that resolution a mountain range becomes a bump, a coastline a stair
step, and a city does not exist. For a question posed at the scale of a catchment or a conurbation,
the raw output is unusable.

Two routes exist. **Dynamical** downscaling runs a fine-grid regional model, taking global outputs
as boundary conditions: the physics is solved, the computing cost is high. **Statistical**
downscaling establishes a relationship between large scale and local observations over the past,
then applies it to the future: it is cheap, but assumes that relationship still holds in a different
climate.

Neither adds information absent from the global model. They redistribute what it contains onto a
finer grid. A highly detailed local projection therefore inherits in full the uncertainty of the
global simulation behind it — spatial detail is not precision.
