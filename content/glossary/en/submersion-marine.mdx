Coastal flooding results from several factors meeting: a high tide, a depression lifting the
sea surface, a wind pushing water shorewards, and a swell overtopping the defences. The mean
level is only the platform they sit on.

Yet it is the mean level that changes everything. A rise of a few tens of centimetres in
<Term id="niveau-marin">sea level</Term> floods nothing by itself, but it shifts the whole
distribution of extreme levels upwards.

<Callout variant="caution" title="The effect on return periods">
An event whose <Term id="periode-de-retour">return period</Term> was one in a hundred years
can become one in ten for a modest rise, because the tail of the distribution is very steep.
That is the mechanism by which a slow rise produces an abrupt break in coastal risk.
</Callout>
