## Why its melting does not raise sea level

Sea ice floats: it already displaces a volume of water equal to its weight. Its
disappearance therefore changes sea level almost not at all, unlike that of an
<Term id="calotte-glaciaire">ice sheet</Term> or a <Term id="glacier">glacier</Term>, which
adds water previously stored on land.

Its climatic significance lies elsewhere, in <Term id="albedo">albedo</Term>: a highly
reflective white surface gives way to a dark ocean that absorbs sunlight. This is one of
the mechanisms behind the Arctic warming markedly faster than the global average.

<Callout variant="info" title="Extent and volume are not the same story">
Monitoring usually reports **extent**, the area covered by at least 15 % ice, because
satellites measure it well. **Thickness** — and therefore volume — is harder to observe and
is declining faster than extent: sea ice can occupy the same area while being distinctly
more fragile.
</Callout>
