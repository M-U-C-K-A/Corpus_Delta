A tsunami is nothing like a wind wave. It sets **the whole water column** in motion, from
surface to sea floor, which explains its energy and why it does not break in open water: out
at sea its amplitude is a matter of tens of centimetres and a ship does not notice it.

It is on approaching the shore, where the depth decreases, that the wave slows and its
amplitude grows abruptly. The term "tidal wave" is a misnomer: the phenomenon has nothing to
do with tides.

A tsunami is not a climate phenomenon. It appears in this glossary under natural hazards,
with which it shares the vocabulary of hazard, exposure and
