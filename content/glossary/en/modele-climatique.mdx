## What a model computes, and what it is given

A model divides the planet into cells of a few tens of kilometres and solves, time step
after time step, the equations of fluid mechanics and radiative transfer. It contains no
curve of future temperature: that comes out of the calculation.

What it is given are the external conditions: gas concentrations, solar activity, volcanic
aerosols. That is exactly what <Term id="scenario-ssp">scenarios</Term> are for.

<Callout variant="caution" title="The grid sets the limits">
Phenomena smaller than a grid cell, such as clouds, storms and ocean eddies, cannot be
computed directly. They are represented by approximate formulas tuned against observations.
That is where the main divergences between models live, and the reason dozens are run rather
than one.
</Callout>

A model is judged first on its ability to reproduce the observed climate of the past, before
any credit is given to its projections.
