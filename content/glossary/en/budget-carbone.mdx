## Why a budget rather than a rate

The warming caused by CO₂ depends essentially on the **cumulative total** of emissions
since the pre-industrial era, not on the pace at which they are produced. This near-linear
relationship between cumulative carbon and temperature is one of the most robust results in
the field.

It has a direct consequence: meeting a temperature goal amounts to not exceeding a total
quantity. Emitting less but for longer changes nothing about the final outcome if the
cumulative total is the same.

<Callout variant="uncertainty" title="A figure that only means something with a probability attached">
A carbon budget makes sense only alongside the warming level targeted and the probability
of holding to it. The budget "for 1.5 °C" at a 50 % chance and the one at 67 % are not the
same number, and the gap is substantial.
</Callout>

Strictly speaking, this budget covers CO₂ alone; the other gases are handled separately,
their timescales being too different to enter the same total.
