## The most common misunderstanding in the vocabulary of risk

A hundred-year flood does not happen "every hundred years". It is an **annual probability**
of 1 %, independent from one year to the next. Two hundred-year floods can occur two years
apart without the statistic being wrong.

Over the thirty years of a mortgage, the probability of experiencing at least one
hundred-year flood exceeds a quarter. Put that way, the same information produces an
entirely different sense of security.

<Callout variant="caution" title="A statistic that assumes a stable climate">
Return periods are computed from past observations, on the assumption that the distribution
of events does not change. It does change. A structure sized for a hundred-year flood using
last century's data now protects against an event that has become more frequent than
planned for.
</Callout>
