## Words with a numerical value

In an <Term id="giec">IPCC</Term> report, "likely" is not a rhetorical hedge. It is a
calibrated term: it means a probability above 66 %. "Very likely" is 90, "virtually certain" 99,
"extremely unlikely" below 5. These terms are set in italics in the reports, precisely so they are
not read as ordinary language.

A second, separate scale qualifies **confidence** in a statement, from very low to very high. It
combines the amount of evidence available with the degree of agreement among it. The two scales
answer different questions: probability concerns a quantified result, confidence the solidity of the
judgement itself.

The distinction matters when reading a synthesis. "High confidence" alongside a wide range signals a
well-established but poorly bounded phenomenon; a narrow range with low confidence signals the
reverse. Press coverage almost always strips these qualifiers, and with them the share of
uncertainty the original text took care to state.
