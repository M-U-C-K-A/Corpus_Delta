## What a glacier gains minus what it loses

A <Term id="glacier">glacier</Term> accumulates snow in winter and loses mass to melt and
sublimation in summer. Mass balance is the algebraic sum of the two over a hydrological year.
Positive, the glacier grows; negative, it thins.

It is expressed in **metres of water equivalent**: the thickness of liquid water the gain or loss
would make if spread over the glacier's whole surface. The unit allows a small Alpine glacier and an
ice cap to be compared, and converts directly into a contribution to
<Term id="niveau-marin">sea level</Term>.

The distinction from glacier length matters: a tongue may still be advancing while the balance has
been negative for years, because ice takes time to flow. Mass balance responds to climate
immediately, front position with a lag of several decades. It is the former, not the latter, that
tells you the state of a glacier.
