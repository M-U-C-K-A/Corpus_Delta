Two very different families go by the same name. Capture **at the smokestack** retains an
industrial plant's CO₂ before it reaches the air: it reduces an emission. **Direct air
capture** extracts CO₂ already present in the atmosphere: it produces a removal, and so a
negative emission.

Only the second can offset residual emissions on a path to
<Term id="neutralite-carbone">carbon neutrality</Term>. The first merely limits what is added.

<Callout variant="uncertainty" title="An assumption still doing heavy lifting in the scenarios">
Most pathways compatible with the Paris Agreement assume substantial recourse to these
techniques in the second half of the century. The IPCC attaches explicit reservations to that
assumption: capacity deployed to date bears no relation to the volumes assumed, and nothing
guarantees the change of scale <Cite id="calvin-2023-ipcc-2023-climate-change-2023" />.
</Callout>
