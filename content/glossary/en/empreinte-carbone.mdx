## Who carries the emission: producer or consumer

Steel made in China and fitted into a car sold in France emits CO₂ in China. Two
accounting systems draw opposite conclusions. The **territorial** inventory, which states report to
the UNFCCC, books the emission to the country where the chimney stands. The carbon footprint, or
**consumption-based** accounting, books it to the country where the product is used.

The gap is not marginal. For most European economies the footprint exceeds the territorial inventory
by roughly 20 to 60 %, because they import more carbon-intensive goods than they export. A country
can therefore post a genuine territorial decline while its footprint stagnates, if it has mainly
relocated production.

Both measures are correct; they answer different questions. The territorial inventory matches what a
state can regulate on its own soil, which makes it the basis for international commitments. The
footprint matches what its demand sets in motion, which makes it the basis for any discussion of
responsibility.
