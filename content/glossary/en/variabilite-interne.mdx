The atmosphere and the ocean exchange heat continuously, following cycles that need no
external cause to exist. <Term id="enso">El Niño and La Niña</Term> are the most visible
example: they redistribute energy already present in the system without adding any.

This variability is why a temperature curve rises in a sawtooth rather than a straight line,
and why comparing two isolated years says nothing about a trend. A decade cooler than the
one before remains perfectly compatible with underlying warming.

<Callout variant="caution" title="The hiatus argument">
Climatologists were long confronted with an apparent slowdown in warming between the late
1990s and the early 2010s. It corresponded to a phase in which the ocean took up more heat,
and it stopped being visible as soon as the observed period was lengthened. Choosing your
endpoints remains the simplest way to make a climate series say anything at all.
</Callout>
