A flood, a heatwave, an earthquake, a cyclone are hazards. The word names the phenomenon
alone, described by its intensity and its probability, saying nothing about its consequences.

This separation is useful because it isolates what the climate changes from what societies
decide. Warming alters the frequency and intensity of certain hazards; it does not directly
alter how many people live on a floodplain.

The hazard is only one of the three terms of <Term id="risque-climatique">risk</Term>, along
with <Term id="exposition">exposure</Term> and
<Term id="vulnerabilite">vulnerability</Term>. An intense hazard with no exposure causes no
damage, which is why disasters of comparable physical magnitude produce human tolls that
bear no relation to one another.
