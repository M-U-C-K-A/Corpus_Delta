Warmer air can hold more water vapour: its capacity to draw moisture out rises with
temperature. This is what is meant by evaporative demand, and it is the mechanism by which
warming worsens <Term id="secheresse">agricultural droughts</Term> even where rainfall does
not decline.

Evapotranspiration consumes energy: as long as soil holds water, part of the incoming
sunlight goes into evaporating it rather than heating the air. When the soil dries out that
safety valve disappears and the same energy goes entirely into warming — the loop that ties
drought to <Term id="canicule">heatwaves</Term>.
