Measuring stations cover neither the oceans, nor the deserts, nor the poles evenly. A
reanalysis fills those gaps by other means than interpolation: it runs a weather model over
the past while continuously correcting it with observations — stations, buoys, ships,
radiosondes, satellites.

The product is neither a pure observation nor a free-running simulation: it is the state of
the atmosphere most consistent with all the measurements **and** with physical law. It is
now the reference tool for studying extremes, because it provides fields that are complete,
regular and homogeneous through time.

<Callout variant="caution" title="Homogeneity is only relative">
The observing network changes over the decades — the arrival of satellites being the
clearest example. A reanalysis inherits those discontinuities: comparing a recent decade
with an old one requires knowing what changed in the input data.
</Callout>
