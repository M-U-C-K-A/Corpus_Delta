The two surviving ice sheets hold most of the planet's fresh water. Melting them entirely
would raise sea level by several tens of metres — a prospect unrelated to human timescales,
but one that gives the measure of what is at stake.

What matters on a century horizon is their **annual contribution**, and above all the
stability of certain sectors. In West Antarctica, part of the ice rests on bedrock that
lies below sea level and slopes inland: a configuration in which retreat, once started,
could continue without further forcing. It is on those grounds that ice sheets feature
among the <Term id="point-de-bascule">tipping points</Term> under study.
