## A local definition, not a universal one

There is no single temperature threshold that defines a heatwave. What counts is the
departure from a place's usual climate: a temperature that is bearable in Seville is an
exceptional event in Oslo, with very real health consequences, because neither bodies nor
buildings nor health services there are prepared for it.

In France, heatwave alerts rest on **département-level** thresholds covering both daytime
and night-time temperatures, set jointly by Météo-France and Santé publique France. The
duration criterion is decisive: it is persistence over several consecutive days that makes
a heatwave, not an isolated peak.

## Why the nights matter as much as the days

The body sheds accumulated heat during the night. When night-time temperatures do not fall
far enough, that recovery does not happen and thermal strain builds from one day to the
next. This is the main reason excess mortality during heatwaves depends so strongly on
night-time minima, and why <Term id="ilot-de-chaleur-urbain">urban heat islands</Term> make
the phenomenon worse.
