## Where the heat actually goes

More than **nine tenths** of the excess energy trapped by greenhouse gases ends up in the
ocean. The atmosphere, which the widely quoted <Term id="anomalie-de-temperature">temperature
anomaly</Term> measures, takes up only about one hundredth.

Heat content sums that energy through the whole thickness of a layer — most often the top two
thousand metres. It is counted in zettajoules, 10²¹ joules, because the magnitudes involved have
no domestic equivalent.

Its value lies in its steadiness. Air temperature swings sharply from year to year with
<Term id="enso">El Niño</Term> and <Term id="variabilite-interne">internal variability</Term>;
ocean heat rises almost without jolts, because water has enormous thermal inertia. That is why it
is the most robust indicator of the planet's energy imbalance, and why the question "has warming
paused?" never arises for it.
