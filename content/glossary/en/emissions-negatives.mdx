## Removing, not emitting less

A negative emission is not an avoided emission. It means taking CO₂ already in the
atmosphere and storing it long enough for the removal to count. Reforestation, soil restoration,
bioenergy with capture, enhanced rock weathering, direct air capture: the methods differ in cost,
land use and storage duration.

They hold a particular place in mitigation pathways, because
<Term id="neutralite-carbone">carbon neutrality</Term> does not assume zero gross emissions: it
assumes whatever remains is offset by an equivalent removal. Any scenario that leaves hard-to-abate
sectors standing — cement, aviation, agriculture — therefore rests on some volume of removal.

The recurring criticism concerns scale. The volumes assumed in many scenarios far exceed what is
demonstrated today, and a hypothetical future removal sometimes serves to justify deferring present
cuts. The two levers are not interchangeable: the second is certain, the first is not.
