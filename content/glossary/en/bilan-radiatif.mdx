This is the most fundamental quantity in the climate system: as long as Earth receives more
energy than it sheds, it warms, whatever the details of atmospheric circulation.

Most of that surplus — around nine tenths — is taken up by the ocean, whose heat capacity
dwarfs the atmosphere's. That is what gives the system its inertia: even at zero emissions,
the heat already stored would go on redistributing itself for decades.
