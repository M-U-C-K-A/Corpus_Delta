## A response that becomes a cause

A <Term id="forcage-radiatif">forcing</Term> comes from outside the system: gas emissions, a
volcanic eruption, a change in solar output. A feedback, by contrast, arises from the
system's own response and adds to the initial perturbation.

The easiest to grasp is the water vapour feedback. Warmer air holds more water vapour, and
water vapour is a greenhouse gas. Warming therefore produces its own strengthening of the
greenhouse effect, which produces warming.

A positive feedback amplifies, a negative one moderates. The word carries no moral weight
here: the positive <Term id="albedo">albedo</Term> feedback makes matters worse, while the
negative feedback of infrared radiation is what prevents runaway warming.

<Callout variant="info" title="Why this governs everything else">
The sum of the feedbacks determines how much temperature rises for a given forcing. That is
precisely what <Term id="sensibilite-climatique">climate sensitivity</Term> measures, and
why it remains the field's most scrutinised parameter.
</Callout>
