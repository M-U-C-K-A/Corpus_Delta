## An engine driven by density

In the North Atlantic, surface water cools, grows denser and sinks. That sinking draws warm
water up from the south, sustaining a loop that carries a considerable amount of heat to
high latitudes — it is part of why Western Europe's climate is milder than its latitude
would suggest.

Fresh water from Greenland's melt lowers surface salinity, hence density, hence the water's
ability to sink. That is the mechanism by which a weakening is envisaged.

<Callout variant="uncertainty" title="A weakening under discussion, not a collapse announced">
The IPCC judges a weakening over the century very likely, but considers a full collapse
unlikely on that horizon. The warnings of imminent shutdown that circulate periodically
rest on isolated studies whose own authors stress their methodological limits.
</Callout>
