## When the body stops recovering

A body tolerates a very hot day if it can cool afterwards. Night serves that purpose:
temperature falls, heart rate eases, accumulated thermal load is shed. When the night minimum stays
above 20 °C, that recovery does not happen.

This is why <Term id="surmortalite">excess mortality</Term> studies give the night minimum at least
as much weight as the daytime maximum. A <Term id="canicule">heatwave</Term> with cool nights kills
markedly fewer people than one of the same maximum with stifling nights. The French warning criteria
accordingly set a threshold on the minimum as much as on the maximum.

Cities are most exposed, because the <Term id="ilot-de-chaleur-urbain">urban heat island</Term>
shows up chiefly at night: concrete and asphalt then release the heat stored during the day. The gap
with the surrounding countryside, modest at noon, can reach several degrees before dawn.
