## A bottled atmosphere

As snow piles up on an <Term id="calotte-glaciaire">ice sheet</Term>, it compacts into ice
and traps air bubbles. Each bubble samples the atmosphere of the moment it sealed. Drilling
vertically therefore means travelling back in time: the EPICA Dome C core in Antarctica spans about
800,000 years.

Two distinct things come out of it. The composition of the trapped air gives past CO₂ and methane
concentrations directly — a measurement, not a reconstruction. The isotopic composition of the ice
itself, in oxygen-18 and deuterium, gives a proxy for the temperature when the snow fell.

This is the source of the most quoted statement in the climate file: today's CO₂ concentration
exceeds anything seen across that whole period. The bubbles say so directly, without passing through
a <Term id="modele-climatique">model</Term>.
