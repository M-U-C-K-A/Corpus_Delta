The main gases affected by human activity are carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide and
the fluorinated gases. Water vapour is the leading natural contributor, but its
concentration is not driven directly by emissions: it adjusts to temperature, which makes
it a **feedback** rather than a forcing.

Two properties set these gases apart: their capacity to absorb infrared radiation, and
their atmospheric lifetime. Methane absorbs far more strongly than CO₂ but disappears
within about a decade; CO₂ absorbs less strongly but a fraction persists for centuries.
Comparing their effects therefore requires specifying over what horizon — which is what
<Term id="potentiel-de-rechauffement-global">global warming potential</Term> answers.
