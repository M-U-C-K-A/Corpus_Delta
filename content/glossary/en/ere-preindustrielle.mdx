## A convention, not a natural date

Industrialisation did not begin on a particular day, and nineteenth-century emissions had
already warmed the planet somewhat by the time reliable measurements appeared. The
1850-1900 window is therefore a compromise: old enough to precede most of the warming,
recent enough to be covered by instrumental records in sufficient numbers.

This convention has a direct bearing on how figures should be read. The same
<Term id="anomalie-de-temperature">temperature departure</Term> does not mean the same thing
depending on whether it is measured against 1850-1900, 1951-1980 or 1991-2020. Agencies
often publish series on different reference periods, and comparing them directly is
misleading until they are brought onto the same base.

The goals of the <Term id="accord-de-paris">Paris Agreement</Term> all refer to this
baseline.
