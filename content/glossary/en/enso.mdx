In an El Niño phase, surface waters in the eastern equatorial Pacific warm abnormally; in a
La Niña phase, they cool. The cycle is irregular, recurring roughly every two to seven
years.

Its effects reach far beyond the Pacific: rainfall regimes in South-East Asia, Australia
and South America, and cyclone activity in the Atlantic.

<Callout variant="info" title="Redistributing is not warming">
ENSO moves heat between ocean and atmosphere; it adds none to the system. El Niño years
stand out as warmer in surface temperature because the ocean gives heat back, not because
Earth has gained energy. This is why El Niño years are compared with one another, rather
than with La Niña years, when judging a trend.
</Callout>
