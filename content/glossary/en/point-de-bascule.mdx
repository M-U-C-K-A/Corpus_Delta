## What the term means — and what it does not

A tipping point is not a threshold of severity: it is a threshold of **reorganisation**.
Below it, a disturbed system returns to its initial state once the disturbance ends; beyond
it, the system moves towards another stable state and stays there, even if the cause
disappears.

The components most often examined in this light are the ice sheets, the North Atlantic
overturning circulation, the Amazon rainforest and permafrost.

<Callout variant="uncertainty" title="A notion often misused">
Where exactly these thresholds lie remains poorly constrained, and the phrase is frequently
used in public debate to mean nothing more than "getting worse". Crossing a threshold means
neither immediate collapse nor global upheaval: the timescales involved run from a century
to a millennium depending on the system.
</Callout>
