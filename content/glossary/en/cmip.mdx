A projection from a single model teaches you little: there is no way to separate what
follows from the physics of the climate from what follows from that particular model's
implementation choices.

CMIP answers this by imposing rigorously identical experiments on independent teams. The
**spread** between their results then becomes information in its own right: it measures the
structural uncertainty that remains, whatever
<Term id="scenario-ssp">scenario</Term> is chosen.

The ranges published by the <Term id="giec">IPCC</Term> come out of this collective
exercise. It is the invisible infrastructure behind most projection charts you will meet.
