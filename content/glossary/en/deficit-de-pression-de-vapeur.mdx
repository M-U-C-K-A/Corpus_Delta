## The atmosphere's thirst

Warm air can hold far more water vapour than cold air — about 7 % more per degree. If the
vapour actually present does not rise as fast, the gap to saturation widens. That gap is the vapour
pressure deficit.

It is the driving force of <Term id="evapotranspiration">evapotranspiration</Term>. The larger it
is, the more water the atmosphere pulls from soils, leaves and dead wood. High VPD therefore dries
forest fuels even without a rainfall deficit, which is why hot summers with normal rain can still see
<Term id="megafeu">megafires</Term>.

VPD has become central to the study of fire and <Term id="secheresse">drought</Term> because it links
warming directly to drying, without going through precipitation, whose trend is far more uncertain.
It accounts for much of the observed increase in burned area across the western United States.
