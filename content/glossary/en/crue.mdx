The distinction between a rise in discharge and a flood is not a nicety of vocabulary: it
separates a hydrological phenomenon from a matter of
<Term id="risque-climatique">risk</Term>. A river spilling into an uninhabited plain is a
high-water event, not a disaster.

The scale of a flood depends as much on the catchment as on the rain: saturated soil
absorbs nothing more, sealed surfaces speed up runoff, a straightened channel passes the
wave downstream faster. This is why the same quantity of water can produce very different
consequences depending on timing and land use — a textbook
<Term id="evenement-compose">compound event</Term>.
