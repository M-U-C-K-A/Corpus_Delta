## The effect that runs the other way

Most combustion aerosols — sulfates in particular — reflect sunlight and **cool** the
surface. They have therefore masked part of the warming caused by greenhouse gases since
the industrial era.

They also act indirectly, by serving as condensation nuclei: more aerosols produce clouds
made of smaller, more numerous droplets, and so more reflective ones. This indirect effect
is the main remaining source of uncertainty about
<Term id="sensibilite-climatique">climate sensitivity</Term>.

<Callout variant="info" title="Cleaning the air warms the planet">
Aerosols remain in the atmosphere for only a few days. Cutting particulate pollution — an
unarguable public-health goal, since these particles kill on a massive scale — therefore
removes that cooling quickly. Both concerns are real, and in the short term they point in
opposite directions.
</Callout>
