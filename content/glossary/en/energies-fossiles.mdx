What these three resources have in common is not their use but their origin: carbon removed
from the <Term id="cycle-du-carbone">cycle</Term> tens or hundreds of millions of years ago
and stored underground ever since. Burning them does not move carbon around inside the
system, it adds carbon to it.

That is what sets them apart from biomass. Burning wood releases carbon captured recently,
which regrowth can recapture. Burning coal releases carbon that no process will put back
underground on any useful timescale.

<Callout variant="info" title="They are not equivalent to one another">
For the same energy delivered, gas emits markedly less CO₂ than coal. But extracting and
transporting it releases methane, whose immediate warming power is far greater. Comparing
the two therefore requires stating the time horizon, which is what
<Term id="potentiel-de-rechauffement-global">global warming potential</Term> answers.
</Callout>
