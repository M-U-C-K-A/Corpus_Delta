## Cap first, allocate second

A carbon market first fixes a total **quantity** of permitted emissions for a given
perimeter — heavy industry, power generation, sometimes aviation. That quantity is cut into
allowances, handed out or auctioned, then tradable between actors.

The logic is that abatement should happen where it costs least. A plant that can cut cheaply does so
and sells its allowances; another, for which cutting would cost more than the allowance, buys. The
total stays capped whatever the distribution, and the cap declines on a schedule set in advance.

The practical difficulty is the level of the cap. Set too generously, it produces a near-zero price
and no effect: this is what the European system experienced for a decade, after an initial
allocation calibrated on activity projections that the 2008 crisis overturned. A carbon market is
worth no more than its cap.
