## Why it is estimated rather than counted

Almost nobody dies with "heat" written on their death certificate. Thermal strain aggravates
existing conditions, and deaths are recorded under their medical cause. Counting the dead of
a <Term id="canicule">heatwave</Term> from declared causes would therefore miss nearly all of
them.

The method is to establish an expected mortality, from the same weeks in previous years
corrected for underlying trends, then to measure the gap with observed mortality. That gap
is the excess mortality.

<Callout variant="caution" title="What the figure does not say">
A statistical estimate names no individual death, which makes it easy to dispute and hard to
defend in public. It nevertheless remains the only available measure, and its order of
magnitude is robust: the same method applied by independent teams yields converging results
