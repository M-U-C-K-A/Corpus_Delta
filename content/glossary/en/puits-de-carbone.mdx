## A status that depends on the balance, not on the nature of the reservoir

A forest, an ocean or a soil exchanges carbon with the atmosphere continuously, in both
directions. What makes it a sink, a source, or neither, is the **net balance** of those
exchanges over the period considered.

That status is therefore not permanent. A forest that burns or endures prolonged water
stress can stop absorbing, or even become a source. This is precisely the concern raised
about several large natural sinks, whose weakening would constitute a
<Term id="point-de-bascule">tipping point</Term>.

The annual estimate of these flows at global scale is the subject of a collective exercise
published each year by the Global Carbon Project.
