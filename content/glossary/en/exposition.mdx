Exposure counts what lies in a <Term id="alea">hazard</Term>'s path: inhabitants, housing,
infrastructure, crops, ecosystems. It says nothing about how fragile they are, only that
they are there.

It is the term of <Term id="risque-climatique">risk</Term> that has grown most over the past
century, for reasons that have nothing to do with climate: urbanisation of coastlines and
floodplains, concentration of population and capital in places that are attractive but
threatened.

<Callout variant="info" title="A methodological piece of good news">
Unlike the hazard, exposure follows from immediate human decisions. It can be reduced
through planning, without waiting for a climate policy to take effect. That is what makes
<Term id="adaptation">adaptation</Term> effective in the short term, where mitigation only
bears fruit on the scale of decades.
</Callout>
