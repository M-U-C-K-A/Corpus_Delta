Urban materials — asphalt, concrete, stone — absorb sunlight during the day and release it
slowly at night. To this are added the heat given off by vehicles, buildings and air
conditioning, and the scarcity of vegetation and water, which deprives the city of
evaporative cooling.

The gap with the surrounding countryside is therefore **largest at night**, precisely when
the body needs to recover. That mismatch is what makes the heat island a factor that
aggravates <Term id="canicule">heatwaves</Term>, rather than a mere summer discomfort.
