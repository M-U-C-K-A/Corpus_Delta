The jet stream owes its existence to the thermal contrast between polar and tropical air.
It does not run straight: it meanders, and those meanders determine which regions receive
cold air, which receive warm air, and where depressions travel.

When a meander stalls, the same weather situation persists for days or weeks over the same
place — drought and <Term id="canicule">heat</Term> on one side, persistent rain on the
other. That persistence is what turns unusual weather into a damaging event.

<Callout variant="uncertainty" title="Still an open question">
As the Arctic warms faster than the tropics, the thermal contrast that feeds the jet
weakens. The hypothesis of wider, more frequently blocked meanders is under active study,
with no consensus so far.
</Callout>
