This is the unit of every emissions inventory and of almost every policy target. It is
obtained by multiplying the mass of a gas by its
<Term id="potentiel-de-rechauffement-global">global warming potential</Term>.

Its convenience has a price: it hides the difference in timescale between gases. Cutting
methane emissions by one tonne of CO₂ equivalent produces a strong, fast effect that then
fades; cutting CO₂ by one tonne produces a more modest but near-permanent one. Both count
as one in the inventory, though they commit the future very differently.
