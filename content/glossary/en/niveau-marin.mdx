## Two causes, not one

The observed rise breaks down into two contributions of comparable weight.
<Term id="dilatation-thermique">Thermal expansion</Term>: warmed water occupies more volume
at constant mass. And the input of mass: water from
<Term id="glacier">glaciers</Term> and <Term id="calotte-glaciaire">ice sheets</Term>
joining the ocean.

<Callout variant="info" title="The level does not rise evenly everywhere">
The global average conceals strong regional disparities: ocean circulation, prevailing
winds, and even the gravitational effects of ice sheets on nearby waters. To which is added
the subsidence of some coastlines, which are sinking — a local phenomenon often faster than
the rise of the sea itself.
</Callout>

For coasts, the decisive parameter is not the annual mean but the **level reached during
storms**: a rise of a few tens of centimetres shifts the frequency of extreme flooding
sharply.
