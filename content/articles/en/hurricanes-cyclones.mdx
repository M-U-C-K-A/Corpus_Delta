# Hurricanes and Cyclones: More Powerful Storms

Tropical cyclones (hurricanes, typhoons) are among the most destructive weather phenomena. Climate change is altering their characteristics in concerning ways.

## Cyclone Fuel

Cyclones draw their energy from evaporation over warm oceans. The warmer the ocean, the higher the energy potential. Minimum surface temperature for formation is about 26-27°C.

## What Observations Show

**Increased intensification**: The proportion of cyclones reaching categories 4-5 (the most intense) has increased by 25-30% since the 1970s.

**Slower movement**: Cyclones are moving more slowly, prolonging their impact on a given area.

**More intense rainfall**: Increased atmospheric moisture generates heavier rains. Hurricane Harvey (2017) dumped more than 1 meter of water on Houston in a few days.

**Poleward extension**: The zone favorable to cyclones is extending north and south.

## Future Projections

Models suggest increased average intensity and proportion of extreme cyclones, even if total numbers may remain stable or slightly decrease.

## Economic Impacts

Damage from major hurricanes now regularly reaches tens of billions of dollars. Hurricane Katrina (2005): $125 billion; Maria (2017): $90 billion; Ian (2022): $110 billion.

## Adaptation

Early warning systems, resilient construction, urban planning avoiding at-risk zones, and restoration of coastal ecosystems (mangroves, reefs) as natural protection.
