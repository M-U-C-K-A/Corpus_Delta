# Ocean Acidification: The Silent Threat

Ocean acidification is a direct consequence of rising carbon dioxide (CO2) levels in the atmosphere. Oceans absorb about 30% of the CO2 emitted by human activities, alarmingly altering their chemistry.

## The Chemical Process

When CO2 dissolves in seawater, it forms carbonic acid. This leads to a decrease in the water's pH, making it more acidic. Since the beginning of the industrial revolution, ocean acidity has increased by 30%.

## Impacts on Marine Wildlife

### Calcifying Organisms
Corals, mollusks (oysters, mussels), and some plankton use calcium carbonate to build their shells and skeletons. Acidification reduces the availability of these minerals, weakening these organisms and threatening their survival.

### Food Chains
Since plankton is at the base of the marine food chain, its decline affects the entire ecosystem, from small fish to large marine mammals like whales.

## Consequences for Humans

Fishing and aquaculture are threatened, impacting food security for millions of people who depend on seafood. Furthermore, the degradation of coral reefs reduces natural coastal protection against storms and affects tourism.

## Acting Now

The only sustainable solution is a drastic reduction in CO2 emissions. In parallel, protecting existing marine ecosystems and reducing other sources of pollution (plastic, fertilizers) can help oceans better withstand this change.
