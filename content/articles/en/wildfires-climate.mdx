# Wildfires: The Age of Megafires

Wildfires are part of many ecosystems, but climate change is transforming this natural phenomenon into unprecedented catastrophes. "Megafires"—over 100,000 acres—have become the new normal.

## Why More Fires?

Warming creates perfect conditions for extreme fires. Higher temperatures dry out vegetation. Prolonged droughts extend "fire seasons." Heat waves create explosive conditions. Forest die-off (drought, insects) produces dead fuel.

## Recent Major Events

**Australia (2019-2020)**: The "Black Summer fires" burned more than 46 million acres, killed 3 billion animals, destroyed 3,000 homes. Fires of unprecedented intensity.

**California (2020-2021)**: Several of the largest fires in state history. The Creek Fire created its own fire storm (pyrocumulonimbus).

**Siberia (2021)**: More than 46 million acres burned, smoke reaching the North Pole.

**Canada (2023)**: Record season with more than 45 million acres, air quality degraded as far as New York.

## Feedback Loop

Fires release CO₂ stored in forests, worsening warming which favors... more fires. Canadian fires in 2023 emitted more CO₂ than all other global fires combined.

## Impacts

Direct deaths and destruction, catastrophic air quality over vast regions, biodiversity loss, post-fire erosion and floods, psychological trauma.

## Solutions

Preventive forest management (clearing, prescribed burns), warning and evacuation systems, fire-resistant construction, and emission reductions to limit worsening.
