# Mangrove Ecosystems: Guardians of Tropical Coasts

Mangroves are coastal forests adapted to saltwater, located on tropical and subtropical shorelines. These exceptional ecosystems play crucial roles in fighting climate change and protecting coasts.

## Distribution and Decline

Mangroves cover approximately **150,000 km²** across 118 countries' coasts. But they have lost 35% of their area since the 1980s due to aquaculture (shrimp), urbanization, and agricultural conversion.

## Ecosystem Services

**Carbon storage**: Mangroves store up to **5 times more carbon per hectare** than tropical terrestrial forests. Much is sequestered in organic-rich soils.

**Coastal protection**: Their tangled roots dissipate wave energy and reduce damage from storms and tsunamis. They also limit erosion.

**Marine nurseries**: 80% of commercial fish species spend part of their lives in mangroves.

**Water filtration**: They retain sediments and pollutants before they reach coral reefs.

## Climate Threats

Sea level rise can submerge mangroves if they cannot migrate inland (often blocked by urbanization). Storm intensification increases damage. Changes in salinity and precipitation affect their health.

## Solutions

Protection of existing mangroves as absolute priority. Restoration of degraded areas. Allowing mangrove "migration" inland by creating buffer zones. Payments for ecosystem services to incentivize local communities to protect them.
