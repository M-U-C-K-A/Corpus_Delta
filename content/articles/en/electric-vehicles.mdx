# Electric Vehicles: The Revolution on Wheels

Electric vehicles (EVs) have gone from niche curiosity to dominant technology in just a few years. In 2023, more than 14 million EVs were sold, representing 18% of global sales.

## Climate Advantages

**Zero emissions at use**: EVs emit no CO₂ while driving. Even with current electricity (global mix), an EV emits 50-70% less CO₂ over its lifecycle than an equivalent combustion vehicle.

**Continuous improvement**: As electricity decarbonizes, the EV advantage automatically increases.

## The Battery Debate

**Manufacturing**: Battery production is energy-intensive and emits CO₂, but this initial "carbon deficit" is repaid after 15,000-30,000 km depending on the electricity mix.

**Materials**: Lithium, cobalt, nickel are needed. Environmental and ethical concerns exist about their extraction. Battery chemistry is evolving to reduce these dependencies (LFP batteries, sodium-ion batteries).

**Recycling**: Batteries can be reused (stationary storage) then recycled. The supply chains are developing.

## Adoption Challenges

**Charging infrastructure**: Need for a dense network of fast chargers.

**Range**: Constantly improving (400-600 km now common).

**Price**: Parity with combustion expected around 2025-2027, already reached in some segments.

## Beyond the Individual Car

EVs are only part of the solution. The transition must be accompanied by electrified public transit, e-bikes and e-scooters, and an overall reduction in travel needs through urban planning.
