# The Carbon Cycle: Foundation of Climate Science

The carbon cycle is one of the most important biogeochemical cycles on our planet. It describes the circulation of carbon between four major reservoirs: the atmosphere, oceans, terrestrial biosphere, and lithosphere.

## Carbon Reservoirs

![Carbon Cycle Diagram](/articles/carbon-cycle.png)

### Atmosphere
The atmosphere contains approximately **870 gigatons of carbon (GtC)** in the form of CO₂. Although the smallest reservoir, it's the most dynamic and directly determines climate.

### Oceans
Oceans store approximately **38,000 GtC**, mainly as dissolved bicarbonates. They absorb about 25% of anthropogenic CO₂ emissions, acting as a major "carbon sink."

### Terrestrial Biosphere
Plants, soils, and organic matter contain approximately **2,000 GtC**. Photosynthesis captures ~120 GtC/year, while respiration and decomposition release an equivalent amount.

### Lithosphere
Sedimentary rocks and fossil fuels store thousands of GtC on geological timescales (millions of years).

## Natural vs Anthropogenic Fluxes

### Natural Fluxes
- **Photosynthesis**: ~120 GtC/year absorbed
- **Respiration and decomposition**: ~120 GtC/year released
- **Ocean-atmosphere exchange**: ~90 GtC/year in each direction

### Anthropogenic Perturbation
Human activities add approximately **10 GtC/year** to the atmosphere:
- Fossil fuels: ~9 GtC/year
- Land use change: ~1 GtC/year

This imbalance accumulates, increasing atmospheric CO₂ concentration by ~2 ppm per year.

## Potential Tipping Points

Certain feedbacks could transform sinks into sources:
- **Tropical forests**: Water stress and fires
- **Permafrost**: Methane and CO₂ release
- **Oceans**: Reduced absorption with warming

## Solutions: Carbon Cycle Management

1. **Reduce fossil emissions**: Energy transition
2. **Protect natural sinks**: Forests, oceans, peatlands
3. **Regenerative agriculture**: Soil sequestration
4. **Capture technologies**: DAC (Direct Air Capture), BECCS
