# Solar Energy: The Silent Revolution

Solar photovoltaics has gone from niche technology to the world's leading source of new electrical capacity. Its exponential growth and plummeting costs make it a pillar of the energy transition.

## Exponential Growth

Global capacity increased 100-fold between 2010 and 2023, from less than 50 GW to over 1,400 GW. In 2023, the world installed more than 400 GW of solar—equivalent to 400 nuclear reactors.

## Cost Collapse

The cost of solar modules has dropped 99% since 1976 and 90% since 2010. Solar is now the cheapest source of electricity in most regions of the world.

## How It Works

Photovoltaic cells directly convert light into electricity through the photoelectric effect in semiconductors (mainly silicon). No moving parts, little maintenance, 25-30 year lifespan.

## Limitations and Solutions

Intermittency (night, clouds) requires storage or complementary sources. Panel production consumes energy and materials, but energy produced over their lifetime is 20-40 times greater.

## Applications

Utility-scale ground installations, residential and commercial rooftops, off-grid systems in rural areas, and building-integrated photovoltaics.

## Perspectives

Projections suggest solar could provide 30-50% of global electricity by 2050, or more in some scenarios.
