# Climate and Health: A Global Health Emergency

The World Health Organization calls climate change "the greatest threat to health in the 21st century." Its impacts already affect millions of people and will worsen.

## Direct Impacts

**Heat waves**: The European heatwave of 2003 caused more than 70,000 premature deaths. Elderly people, outdoor workers, and urban populations are most vulnerable. Heat stress also reduces productivity and cognitive abilities.

**Extreme events**: Floods, storms, and fires cause deaths, injuries, psychological trauma, and population displacement.

**Air quality**: Warming amplifies ozone pollution, worsens allergies (more abundant and early pollen), and wildfires degrade air quality across entire regions.

## Indirect Impacts

**Vector-borne diseases**: Dengue, malaria, Lyme disease, and other mosquito- and tick-borne diseases are expanding their geographic range. Europe is already seeing indigenous dengue cases.

**Waterborne diseases**: Floods and droughts favor cholera, typhoid, and other diseases related to contaminated water.

**Food security**: Malnutrition linked to declining agricultural yields and rising food prices.

**Mental health**: Eco-anxiety, post-traumatic stress after disasters, and psychological impacts of forced displacement.

## Vulnerable Populations

Children, elderly, chronically ill, pregnant women, outdoor workers, disadvantaged populations, and low-income countries are disproportionately affected.

## Solutions

Heat wave warning systems, urban greening to reduce heat islands, resilient health systems, and climate action as public health policy. The WHO estimates limiting warming would save millions of lives.
