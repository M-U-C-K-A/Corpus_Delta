# Climate Refugees: Displacement from Warming

Climate change is already pushing millions of people to leave their homes. By 2050, this number could reach 200 million. Yet these "climate refugees" have no international legal status.

## Causes of Displacement

**Sudden events**: Floods, storms, fires force immediate evacuations. Often temporary, they can become permanent.

**Gradual degradation**: Repeated droughts, desertification, salinization of agricultural land make regions eventually uninhabitable.

**Sea level rise**: Small island states and densely populated deltas are the first threatened.

## Current Numbers

Each year, about **20-25 million people** are displaced by climate-related disasters. The Internal Displacement Monitoring Centre documents these movements, mostly internal (within their own country).

## Most Affected Regions

South and Southeast Asia (Bangladesh, Philippines, India), Sub-Saharan Africa (Sahel, Horn of Africa), Central America (dry corridor), and small Pacific island states.

## Lack of Legal Status

The 1951 Geneva Convention on refugees doesn't recognize environmental causes. "Climate refugees" don't have access to international protections reserved for political refugees.

## Solutions

In-place adaptation to prevent displacement, planning for organized migration when necessary, creating an international legal status, funding adaptation in vulnerable countries, and climate action to limit worsening.
