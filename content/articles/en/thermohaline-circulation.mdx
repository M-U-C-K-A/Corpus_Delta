# Thermohaline Circulation: The Global Ocean Engine

The thermohaline circulation, also called the "Global Ocean Conveyor Belt," is a global system of ocean currents driven by differences in water density, themselves caused by variations in temperature ("thermo") and salinity ("haline").

## Operating Mechanism

![Global Thermohaline Circulation](/articles/thermohaline-circulation.png)

1.  **Heat Transport**: Warm surface currents (like the Gulf Stream) transport heat from the tropics to high latitudes, moderating Western Europe's climate.
2.  **Deep Water Formation**: In the North Atlantic, water cools, becomes saltier (as sea ice formation excludes salt), and thus denser. It then sinks to the abyss: this is "deep convection."
3.  **Return to Surface**: These cold waters travel along the ocean floor and upwell centuries later in the Pacific and Indian Oceans.

## The Weakening of AMOC

The AMOC (Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation) is the Atlantic branch of this system. Observations and models indicate a slowdown of the AMOC by about 15% since the mid-20th century.

### Causes of Slowdown
Global warming disrupts North Atlantic water density through:
*   **Warming Waters**: Warm water is less dense.
*   **Freshwater Input**: Accelerated melting of the Greenland Ice Sheet and increased precipitation inject freshwater at the surface. Since freshwater is less dense than saltwater, this prevents deep water sinking.

## Potential Consequences

A collapse of the AMOC, although considered unlikely before 2100 by the IPCC, would have cataclysmic consequences:
*   Abrupt cooling of Europe.
*   Shifts in African and Asian monsoons.
*   Rapid sea-level rise on the US East Coast.
*   Major disruption of marine ecosystems and fish stocks.
