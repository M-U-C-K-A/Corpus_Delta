# Smart Grids and Storage: The Intermittency Challenge

Solar and wind are intermittent: they only produce when the sun shines or wind blows. Smart grids and energy storage are keys to a 100% renewable electricity system.

## The Intermittency Challenge

With 30-50% variable renewables, the grid can generally adapt. Beyond that, solutions are needed to balance supply and demand at all times.

## Flexibility Solutions

**Interconnections**: Linking different regions smooths production (it's always windy somewhere).

**Demand management**: Charge electric vehicles or run certain industries when electricity is abundant.

**Peaking plants**: Gas turbines (transitionally) or hydrogen for periods of underproduction.

## Storage Technologies

**Lithium-ion batteries**: Short-term storage (hours). Costs plummeting. Ideal for daily smoothing.

**Pumped hydro storage (PHS)**: Pump water to a higher reservoir. 90% of global storage. Requires suitable geography.

**Hydrogen**: Long-term storage (days to weeks). Efficiency loss but high capacity.

**Flow batteries**: Medium-duration stationary storage.

## Smart Grids

Smart grids use sensors and algorithms to balance production and consumption in real time, and integrate millions of decentralized sources (solar panels, vehicle batteries).

## Perspectives

A 100% renewable system is technically possible but requires massive investment in grids, storage, and demand flexibility.
