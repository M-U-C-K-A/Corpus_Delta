# Radiative Forcing: The Energy Accounting of Climate

Radiative forcing (RF) is the standardized measure of different factors' influence on Earth's energy balance. It's the central tool the IPCC uses to compare contributions to climate change.

## Definition

![Radiative Forcing Contributions](/articles/radiative-forcing.png)

Radiative forcing is defined as the net change in energy flux (in W/m²) at the tropopause caused by an external factor, before the system has had time to adjust.

- **Positive RF** → Warming (more incoming energy)
- **Negative RF** → Cooling (more outgoing energy)

## Contributions by Source (1750-2019)

### Positive Forcing (Warming)

| Factor | RF (W/m²) | Uncertainty |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| CO₂ | +2.16 | ±0.22 |
| CH₄ | +0.54 | ±0.11 |
| N₂O | +0.21 | ±0.03 |
| Halocarbons | +0.41 | ±0.08 |
| Tropospheric ozone | +0.47 | ±0.23 |
| **Total GHG** | **+3.84** | - |

### Negative Forcing (Cooling)

| Factor | RF (W/m²) | Uncertainty |
|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Aerosols (direct) | -0.22 | ±0.35 |
| Aerosols (cloud) | -0.84 | ±0.68 |
| Surface albedo change | -0.12 | ±0.10 |
| **Total cooling** | **-1.3** | - |

### Other Factors

- **Solar**: +0.05 W/m² (minor contribution)
- **Aviation contrails**: +0.06 W/m²

## Net Forcing

**Net anthropogenic RF (2019) ≈ +2.72 W/m²**

This is equivalent to ~1% of absorbed solar flux, or about **450 terawatts** of extra power continuously heating Earth.

## From RF to Temperature

The relationship between forcing and temperature is given by climate sensitivity:

**ΔT ≈ λ × RF**

Where λ ≈ 0.8 °C/(W/m²) (typical value)

So RF = +2.72 W/m² → +2.2°C at equilibrium (of which ~1.1°C already realized)

## Policy Implications

The RF concept allows us to:
1. **Compare** impacts of different emissions
2. **Calculate** remaining carbon budgets
3. **Evaluate** policy effectiveness
4. **Understand** "committed warming" from past emissions
