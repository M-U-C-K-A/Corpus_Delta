# The IPCC: The Voice of Global Climate Science

The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) is the reference body for assessing climate science. Its reports guide global policy decisions.

## What is the IPCC?

Created in 1988 by the UN (WMO and UNEP), the IPCC doesn't conduct research: it synthesizes thousands of existing scientific studies to establish the state of knowledge.

The IPCC has 195 member countries. Its reports are approved line by line by government representatives, giving them unique political legitimacy.

## The Three Working Groups

**Working Group I**: The physical science basis of climate change. Temperature, precipitation, sea level, extreme events.

**Working Group II**: Impacts, adaptation, and vulnerability. Consequences for ecosystems, agriculture, health, societies.

**Working Group III**: Mitigation of climate change. How to reduce emissions, technologies, policies.

## Report Cycles

Approximately every 6-7 years, the IPCC publishes a complete report cycle. The latest (AR6) was published in 2021-2023.

Each report undergoes a rigorous review process with thousands of comments from scientists and governments.

## Key IPCC Conclusions

Warming is unequivocal. Human activities are the dominant cause. Impacts are worsening. Limiting warming to 1.5°C is still possible but requires unprecedented transformations.

## Criticisms and Responses

Some accuse the IPCC of alarmism. In reality, the consensus process tends toward the opposite: IPCC projections have often been exceeded by observations (ice melt, sea level).
