# Food Security: Feeding 10 Billion Under a Changing Climate

The global food system must feed a growing population while facing climate disruptions. The challenge is immense: production must increase as conditions become more difficult.

## Impacts on Crops

**High temperatures**: Beyond certain thresholds, yields drop sharply. Corn loses 7% yield per degree above 30°C average during flowering.

**Droughts**: Water deficits reducing yields, sometimes to zero during extreme episodes.

**Extreme events**: Floods, hail, late frosts destroying harvests.

**Pests and diseases**: Geographic expansion and intensification with warming.

**CO₂ and nutrition**: High CO₂ levels reduce protein and micronutrient content in cereals.

## Projections

Without adaptation, wheat, corn, and rice yields could drop 2-6% per decade in tropical and subtropical regions. Some northern regions may temporarily benefit from longer seasons.

## Prices and Access

Climate shocks to production translate into food price spikes, affecting the poorest first. The food riots of 2008 and 2011 were linked to price peaks.

## Solutions

Heat and drought-resistant varieties, efficient irrigation, crop and supply diversification, reducing losses and waste (30-40% of production is lost), and transition to less resource-intensive diets.
