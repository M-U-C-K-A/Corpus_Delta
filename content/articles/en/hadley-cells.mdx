# Hadley Cells: The Engine of Atmospheric Circulation

The general circulation of the atmosphere is organized into large convection cells that redistribute heat from the tropics to the poles. Understanding these cells is essential for grasping climate zones and warming impacts.

## Structure of Global Circulation

![Atmospheric Circulation Cells](/articles/hadley-cells.png)

The atmosphere is divided into three cells per hemisphere:

### Hadley Cell (0° - 30°)
The most powerful of the three cells:
1. **Equatorial ascending**: Warm, moist air rises near the equator (ITCZ - Intertropical Convergence Zone), creating abundant rainfall.
2. **Poleward flow aloft**: Air moves toward the subtropics.
3. **Subtropical subsidence**: Air descends around 30°, creating high-pressure zones and major deserts (Sahara, Kalahari, Sonora).
4. **Trade winds**: Air returns to the equator at the surface, deflected by the Coriolis force.

### Ferrel Cell (30° - 60°)
An indirect cell, "driven" by neighboring cells:
- Dominant westerly winds
- Variable temperate climate
- Air mass confrontation zone

### Polar Cell (60° - 90°)
A direct thermal cell but weak:
- Subsidence at the pole (polar anticyclone)
- Polar easterlies

## Climate Change Impact

Warming modifies these cells in several ways:

### Hadley Cell Expansion
Observations since 1979:
- Widening by ~0.2-0.5° latitude per decade
- Extension of subtropical arid zones
- Shifting rain belts

### Circulation Weakening
- Reduced equator-pole gradient
- Potential cell slowdown
- Monsoon impacts

## Regional Consequences

| Region | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| Mediterranean | Increasing aridity |
| Sahel | Northward shift |
| Australia | Drought extension |
| US Midwest | Precipitation changes |

## Solutions and Adaptation

1. **Water management** in zones becoming arid
2. **Adapted agriculture** to new precipitation regimes
3. **Emission reduction** to limit Hadley expansion
