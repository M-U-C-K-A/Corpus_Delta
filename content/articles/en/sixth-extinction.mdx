# Sixth Mass Extinction: The Crisis of Life

Earth's history has seen five mass extinctions, the most famous of which eliminated the dinosaurs 66 million years ago. Today, scientists believe we have entered a sixth extinction, this time caused by humanity.

## Evidence of Extinction

The current extinction rate is estimated at **100 to 1000 times higher** than the natural background rate. Since 1970, vertebrate populations have declined by **69%** on average (WWF Living Planet Index). One million species are threatened with extinction (IPBES, 2019).

## Main Causes

**Habitat destruction**: Deforestation, urbanization, and intensive agriculture are the primary drivers of extinction.

**Overexploitation**: Unsustainable hunting, fishing, and collection.

**Invasive species**: Introduced by humans, they eliminate native species.

**Pollution**: Pesticides, plastics, and chemical contaminants.

**Climate change**: Increasingly important, it forces species to migrate or adapt faster than they can.

## The Role of Climate Change

Warming modifies species ranges, desynchronizes ecological relationships (pollinators and plants, prey and predators), and amplifies existing stresses. Mountain and polar species are particularly vulnerable as they have "nowhere to go."

## Ecosystem Services at Risk

Biodiversity isn't just aesthetic: it provides pollination (75% of crops), water purification, climate regulation, fertile soils, and medicines.

## Solutions

Protection of 30% of land and sea by 2030, restoration of degraded ecosystems, regenerative agriculture, ending overexploitation, and ambitious climate action.
