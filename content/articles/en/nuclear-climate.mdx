# Nuclear Energy and Climate: An Unavoidable Debate

Nuclear energy is one of the lowest CO₂-emitting electricity sources. Yet its role in fighting climate change remains hotly debated.

## Nuclear's Carbon Footprint

Over the entire life cycle (construction, uranium extraction, operation, decommissioning), nuclear emits about **12 gCO₂/kWh**—comparable to wind, 50 times less than gas, 100 times less than coal.

## Global Situation

Nuclear provides about **10% of global electricity** and about 25% of low-carbon electricity. France, with 70% nuclear electricity, has one of the most decarbonized electricity mixes in the world.

## Favorable Arguments

Dispatchable energy available 24/7 is a major asset. Nuclear complements intermittent renewables. Land footprint is small and energy very dense.

## Unfavorable Arguments

Accident risks (Chernobyl, Fukushima) are real but rare. Managing radioactive waste for millennia raises questions. Construction costs and timelines often overrun. Nuclear proliferation remains a concern.

## New Technologies

SMRs (Small Modular Reactors), Generation IV reactors using less uranium and producing less waste, and long-term nuclear fusion are attracting interest.

## Climate Expert Position

Most IPCC scenarios limiting warming to 1.5°C include some nuclear. It's not the only solution, but it can contribute to the low-carbon mix.
