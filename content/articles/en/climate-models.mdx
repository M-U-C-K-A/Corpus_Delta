# Climate Modeling: Simulating Digital Earth

Climate models are mathematical representations of Earth's climate system. These tools, run on supercomputers, are essential for understanding past, present, and future climate.

## Structure of a Climate Model

![Climate Model Architecture](/articles/climate-models.png)

### Coupled Components
A modern climate model (ESM - Earth System Model) couples:
1. **Atmosphere**: Dynamics, thermodynamics, chemistry
2. **Ocean**: Circulation, temperature, salinity
3. **Cryosphere**: Sea ice, ice sheets, permafrost
4. **Terrestrial biosphere**: Vegetation, carbon cycle
5. **Marine biosphere**: Phytoplankton, biological pump

### Spatial Discretization
Earth is divided into a 3D grid:
- **Horizontal resolution**: 50-250 km (typical)
- **Vertical resolution**: 30-80 atmosphere levels, 40-100 ocean levels
- **Time step**: Minutes to hours

### Equations Solved
Models numerically solve equations for:
- Navier-Stokes (fluid dynamics)
- Radiative transfer
- Thermodynamics
- Mass conservation

## Sub-grid Process Parameterization

Processes smaller than grid cells are "parameterized":
- Clouds and precipitation
- Turbulence
- Convection
- Ocean waves

This is the main source of uncertainty between models.

## Model Validation

### Tests on Current Climate
Do models correctly reproduce:
- Global average temperature ✓
- Precipitation distribution ✓
- Ocean circulation ✓
- Variability (ENSO, NAO) ✓

### Paleoclimate Tests
Do models correctly simulate:
- Last Glacial Maximum ✓
- Mid-Holocene ✓
- Eocene ✗ (difficulty with very warm climates)

## Limitations and Improvements

### Current Limitations
- Insufficient resolution for local phenomena
- Uncertainty about cloud feedbacks
- Tipping points poorly represented

### Future Developments
- Kilometer-scale resolution (convection-permitting models)
- Machine learning for parameterizations
- Exascale computing
