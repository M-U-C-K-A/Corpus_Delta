Climate projections are almost always presented as bundles of trajectories tagged with an
acronym. Understanding what those labels cover changes the way the resulting charts read.

## A model does not guess the future of societies

The physics of the atmosphere and the ocean is simulated from known laws. But the path of
emissions depends on political, economic and demographic decisions that no physical model
can anticipate.

The chosen solution is to make those assumptions explicit rather than hide them: they are
the <Term id="scenario-ssp">shared socio-economic pathways</Term>, described in detail by
the community that built them
<Cite id="riahi-2016-shared-socioeconomic-pathways-their-energy" />.

## Decoding the label

The label `SSP2-4.5` carries two distinct pieces of information.

The first digit denotes the socio-economic narrative: `1` a world oriented towards
sustainability, `2` a continuation of current trends, `3` regional fragmentation, `5` rapid
growth built on fossil fuels.

The decimal number gives the <Term id="forcage-radiatif">radiative forcing</Term> reached in
2100, in watts per square metre. The higher it is, the greater the energy imbalance imposed
on the system.

<Callout variant="caution" title="SSP5-8.5 is not the business-as-usual case">
This scenario is routinely presented as the path followed in the absence of action. It was
in fact designed as an **upper bound**, meant to test how models respond, and assumes a
reliance on coal that observed trends do not support. Using it as the default reference
distorts the reading in both directions.
</Callout>

## Why several models rather than one

A projection from a single model teaches you little: there would be no way to separate what
follows from the physics of the climate from what follows from that particular model's
implementation choices.

The CMIP protocol answers this by running dozens of independent models through rigorously
identical experiments
<Cite id="eyring-2016-overview-coupled-model-intercomparison-project" />. The spread between
their results then becomes information in its own right: it measures the structural
uncertainty that remains, whatever scenario is chosen.

It is from this collective exercise that the ranges published by the IPCC come
<Cite id="calvin-2023-ipcc-2023-climate-change-2023" />.

## Three uncertainties that are not alike

A projection accumulates uncertainties of different kinds, and it helps not to confuse them.

**Scenario** uncertainty depends on human decisions: science cannot reduce it, only
collective choices can. **Model** uncertainty comes from the approximations needed to
represent complex processes, clouds above all; it shrinks as modelling improves. **Internal
variability** stems from the system's chaotic character: it dominates in the short term and
at small spatial scales, and fades over the long run.

<Term id="sensibilite-climatique">Climate sensitivity</Term> belongs to the second category.
It is the parameter by which an emissions assumption is converted into a
<Term id="anomalie-de-temperature">temperature anomaly</Term>.
