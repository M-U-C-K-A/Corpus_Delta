A "global mean temperature" exists nowhere as a direct measurement. It is a reconstructed
quantity, and understanding how it is reconstructed disposes of most of the objections
raised against it.

## The problem with stations

The observing network has never been regular. Stations cluster in inhabited, wealthy
regions; they have moved, changed instruments, and watched their surroundings become urban.
Crudely averaging their readings would produce a figure governed by that geography rather
than by the climate.

The solution is a shift of object: we do not average temperatures, we average
<Term id="anomalie-de-temperature">anomalies</Term> — each station's departure from its own
normal. Anomalies behave coherently across wide regions where absolute temperatures do not,
which makes it possible to interpolate between stations without inventing anything.

<Callout variant="info" title="The check by independence">
Several teams — NASA, NOAA, the Met Office, Berkeley Earth — separately produce a global
series, with different homogenisation and interpolation methods. Their results agree. It is
that convergence between independent approaches, more than the quality of any one method,
that grounds confidence in the signal.
</Callout>

## Reanalyses: filling the gaps with physics

Stations do not cover the oceans, the deserts or the poles evenly. A **reanalysis** answers
this by running a weather model over the past, constraining it continuously with every
available observation — stations, buoys, ships, radiosondes, satellites.

The result is neither a pure observation nor a free-running simulation: it is the state of
the atmosphere most consistent with all the measurements and with physical law. These
products have become the reference for studying extremes, because they provide complete,
regular fields <Cite id="munoz-sabater-2021-era5-land-state-art-global" />.

## Measuring directly what can be measured

Not every quantity requires that reconstruction. Atmospheric CO₂ concentration is measured
directly, at sites chosen far from local sources, and its record is among the most reliable
in climate science. That is what makes it the anchor point of the
<Term id="forcage-radiatif">radiative balance</Term> and of the carbon budget
<Cite id="friedlingstein-2023-global-carbon-budget-2023" />.

## Observation and modelling are not opposed

Observations serve to build models, to evaluate them, and to constrain their parameters —
foremost among them <Term id="sensibilite-climatique">climate sensitivity</Term>.
Intercomparison protocols systematically confront simulations with observations over the
historical period before granting any credit to their projections
<Cite id="eyring-2016-overview-coupled-model-intercomparison-project" />.
