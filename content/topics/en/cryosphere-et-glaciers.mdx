The cryosphere — glaciers, ice sheets, sea ice, snow, frozen ground — is the component of
the climate system that responds most visibly to warming. It is also the one that amplifies
it most directly.

## An indicator that is hard to dispute

Unlike a mean temperature, which results from statistical treatment, the retreat of a
glacier is an observable geometric fact. An analysis based on satellite imagery established
a mass balance for nearly every glacier in the world, and showed that the loss has
**accelerated** over the first two decades of the twenty-first century
<Cite id="hugonnet-2021-accelerated-global-glacier-mass-loss" />.

That acceleration tracks the evolution of the global
<Term id="anomalie-de-temperature">temperature anomaly</Term> closely.

## Amplification through albedo

Ice and fresh snow reflect a large share of sunlight; ocean and bare rock absorb it. When
ice disappears, the uncovered surface captures more energy, which sharpens local warming and
accelerates the melt.

This <Term id="albedo">albedo</Term> feedback is one of the reasons the Arctic warms
markedly faster than the global average. It illustrates a general point: the climate system
responds not only to the initial perturbation, but also to its own responses.

## Permafrost, a reservoir in waiting

<Term id="permafrost">Permafrost</Term> holds, frozen, a considerable quantity of organic
matter accumulated over millennia. Cold has halted microbial decomposition there; thaw
restarts it, releasing carbon dioxide and methane
<Cite id="schuur-2015-climate-change-permafrost-carbon-feedback" />.

<Callout variant="uncertainty" title="A feedback that is hard to quantify">
The scale and timing of that release remain poorly constrained. Thaw is not uniform, some of
the carbon may be taken back up by vegetation colonising the thawed ground, and the split
between CO₂ and methane — whose effects differ sharply — depends on local moisture
conditions. This is why projections still incorporate the term imperfectly.
</Callout>

That very uncertainty is what earns permafrost a place among the possible
<Term id="point-de-bascule">tipping points</Term>: not because a tipping is established, but
because it cannot be ruled out
<Cite id="calvin-2023-ipcc-2023-climate-change-2023" />.
