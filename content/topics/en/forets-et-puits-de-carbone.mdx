About half the CO₂ emitted each year by human activity does not stay in the atmosphere.
The ocean takes one share, terrestrial ecosystems another. That second service is largely
rendered by forests — and it is guaranteed neither in size nor in duration.

## A sink, a stock, a flux

Three notions are often conflated in debate, and separating them avoids most of the
misreadings.

The **stock** is the carbon already held in a forest's biomass and soils. An old forest
holds a great deal of it, even if it absorbs almost nothing further: it is at equilibrium,
what it captures balancing what decomposition releases.

The **flux** is what it exchanges with the atmosphere each year. It can be positive or
negative depending on season, tree age, the dryness of the year.

The **sink** is the net flux over time. A young growing plantation is an active sink with a
small stock; a primary forest is the reverse. Felling the second to plant the first
destroys a certain stock to acquire a temporary sink — the trade is not favourable, and yet
it is regularly presented as though it were.

<Callout variant="info" title="Why stock matters more than flux">
A sink can stop. A released stock is immediate damage. That asymmetry is what grounds the
priority the literature gives to protecting existing forests over new planting.
</Callout>

## A service rendered, and its fragility

The world's forests absorb, net, a considerable volume of carbon, and the most recent
synthesis finds this sink broadly stable over recent decades — but with sharply diverging
regional trajectories behind the average.

<Cite id="pan-2024-enduring-world-forest-carbon-sink" />

That overall stability masks a local reversal. Repeated airborne measurements over
Amazonia show that the south-east of the basin, the most deforested and the driest, has
become a net **source** of carbon: it releases more than it captures.

<Cite id="gatti-2021-amazonia-carbon-source-linked-deforestation" />

Two mechanisms reinforce each other. Deforestation directly removes stock and reduces the
moisture recycling on which downwind rainfall depends. Drought and heat, for their part,
raise tree mortality: most species live with a narrow hydraulic safety margin, and that
margin varies little from one biome to another.

## What an offset tonne is worth

The <Term id="compensation-carbone">offsetting</Term>-by-planting argument assumes that a
tonne stored in a tree cancels a tonne emitted by combustion. Four conditions are needed
for the equivalence to hold, and they are rarely all met.

**Additionality**: the forest would not have grown without the funding. Many projects pay
for conservation that was happening anyway.

**Permanence**: the carbon must stay stored as long as it would have stayed in the
atmosphere, that is, centuries. A fire, a felling or a change of owner is enough to void
the credit — and warming makes precisely those events more likely.

**No leakage**: the pressure to exploit must not simply have moved elsewhere.

**Counting the right carbon**: fossil carbon had been out of the cycle for millions of
years. Burying it in living biomass, which will recycle it within decades, is not a
symmetrical operation.

<Callout variant="warn" title="What this does not imply">
None of the above invalidates protecting or restoring forests, which deliver considerable
benefits — biodiversity, water, soils, preserved stock. What is at issue is the accounting
equivalence with a fossil tonne, not the value of the action.
</Callout>

## Forests in the global balance

The annual carbon budget published by the Global Carbon Project quantifies land-use-change
emissions and terrestrial sink uptake separately. Both items carry the largest
uncertainties in the whole balance: they rest on heterogeneous national inventories and on
vegetation models, not on a direct measurement comparable to that of atmospheric CO₂.

<Cite id="friedlingstein-2026-global-carbon-budget-2025" />

That is a reason for caution about regional figures, and a further reason not to rest a
mitigation strategy on the least well measured item in the balance.
